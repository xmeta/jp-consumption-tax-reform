= Candidate-person Coverage Lemma
:revdate: 2026-09-08
:stem: latexmath

== Purpose

The stage-1 denominator is intentionally not a fitted pseudo-filer count.
It is the entire 2020 Census population, `126,146,099` persons.

This makes the denominator loose but permits a simple coverage argument.

== Lemma

For the static 2020 Census frame, let stem:[T] be the number of individual
income-tax candidate persons relevant to the filing-participation calculation
and stem:[P] the total Census population.

Because Japanese self-assessment income tax is filed by individual persons,
and every in-frame candidate used here must correspond to a person in that
population,

[stem]
++++
T \le P = 126,146,099.
++++

Therefore replacing the unknown stem:[T] with stem:[P] can only weakly lower
the filing-participation lower bound.

== Scope

This lemma does not claim that every Census person is a plausible filer.
Children, institutional residents and many other persons are deliberately
included in stem:[P].  Their inclusion makes the denominator more
conservative.

The lemma also does not place persons outside the static Census frame into
stem:[P].  Post-Census births and international inflows are instead handled
adversarially as contamination in script 47.

Non-resident final-return filers who are outside the resident/Census bridge are
represented by the unresolved contamination parameter stem:[C_{NR}].

== Consequence

The former open condition "prove candidate tax-unit coverage" is closed for
this conservative person-denominator formulation.

The remaining identification problem is not denominator coverage but the upper
bound on frame-incompatible final-return filers, dominated by stem:[C_{NR}].

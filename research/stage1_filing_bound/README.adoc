= F71561 Stage-1 Filing Participation: Partial-Identification Frontier
:revdate: 2026-09-08
:stem: latexmath

== Status

This module strengthens the first-stage filing-participation result without
promoting `income_tax` to READY.

The current result is a *robustness / identification frontier*, not a point
estimate of the filing rate.

== Population-overlap bound

Official aggregate counts imply:

* conservative F71561 mother-population person lower bound: `122,494,621`
* conservative domestic out-of-mother upper bound: `3,651,478`
* conditional return-filer overlap lower bound: `19,438,597`
* candidate tax-unit upper bound: `126,146,099`
* zero-extra-contamination stage-1 lower bound: `15.4096%`

The recovered V6 threshold for `score_power=2.0` is `2.20%`.
With no additional out-of-frame contamination, erasing that rejection requires
`16,663,383` filer records.

== Formal contamination frontier

Let stem:[F] be NTA final-return filers, stem:[P] the conservative candidate-person upper
bound, stem:[M_L] the F71561 mother-person lower bound, and stem:[C] additional filer
records not represented by the population bridge.

[stem]
++++
B_0 = \max\{0, F-(P-M_L)\},
\qquad
L(C)=\frac{\max\{0,B_0-C\}}{P}.
++++

For a prior threshold stem:[A], the prior is rejected whenever

[stem]
++++
C < C^*(A)=B_0-PA.
++++

For `score_power=2.0`, stem:[C^*=16,663,382.822].

== Adversarially tracked contamination

To avoid optimistic assumptions, script 47 treats all of the following as
potential contamination even though this massively overcounts actual filers:

* every birth in 2020--2024: `3,836,677`;
* every registered international inflow in 2020--2024: `2,643,204`;
* every death in 2024 as if it generated one quasi-final return: `1,605,378`.

The sum is `8,085,259`.  Categories can overlap and full-year 2020 births and
inflows include events before the 2020-10-01 Census; both features deliberately
make this an upper bound rather than a fitted estimate.

After allocating this entire amount adversarially, `score_power=2.0` still
requires a further `8,578,124` unresolved filer records (6.8001% of the
candidate-person upper bound) to erase the rejection.

This residual is primarily a target for bounding non-resident and other
population-definition mismatches.

== Why the result is still not READY

The conservative candidate-person denominator coverage condition is closed by
link:candidate_person_coverage.adoc[candidate_person_coverage.adoc]: individual
income-tax candidates in the static Census frame cannot exceed the total
Census population used as the denominator upper bound.

The principal remaining bridge is to bound NTA final-return filers outside the
resident/Census-compatible frame, especially non-resident filers.  The public
source audit is recorded in
link:nonresident_source_audit.adoc[nonresident_source_audit.adoc].

Until that bridge is closed, `income_tax = NOT_READY` remains unchanged.

== Survey-frame facts

The 2024 National Survey of Family Income, Consumption and Wealth sample design
uses 2020 Census results rearranged to 2024 municipality boundaries.  It
explicitly excludes Wajima, Suzu, Anamizu and Noto from the mother-population
estimate.

The mother population includes ordinary two-plus households and eligible
single persons.  Foreign-national households are included in the mother
population, although they are excluded from actual survey targeting because
their household income and expenditure are difficult to measure consistently.

Table 4 reports `21,142,885` one-person general households, `602,771`
students and `20,540,114` included single-person households.

= VAT policy scenario integration matrix
:revdate: 2026-09-10

== Purpose

This adapter satisfies the integration step of Issue #26 without inventing
unidentified macro effects. It maps the existing VAT institutional
compliance/allocation stress module into all eight requested policy scenarios
and reports GDP, growth, distribution, poverty, inflation, fiscal and debt
outcome statuses in the same rows.

It is deliberately an *integration layer*, not a replacement macro model.

== Scenarios

The first three scenarios retain VAT administration. The last five use the same
full-abolition institutional stress grid and differ only by financing strategy.
Financing labels must not alter the compliance/allocation component.

== Numeric versus unresolved outputs

The adapter numerically carries:

* the model-contingent institutional GDP level component
  stem:[m(\rho c_{VAT}+a_{alloc})];
* its annualized transition-growth contribution; and
* a static FY2024 central-government VAT cash-receipt replacement reference.
The stress range remains *not an empirical bound*. Current evidence does not
identify national stem:[c_{VAT}], stem:[\rho], or stem:[a_{alloc}].

The fiscal reference is parsed from the Ministry of Finance FY2024 treasury
revenue table.  Consumption-tax cash receipts are 25,021,206,715 thousand yen,
or 25.021206715 trillion yen.  Total tax receipts are 74.187865765 trillion yen,
so consumption tax represents about 33.73% of the recorded central tax receipts.
For the 0% and full-abolition scenarios this amount is carried as a *static gross
receipt-removal/replacement reference*.  For the JGB scenario the same amount is
shown as a one-for-one gross JGB financing reference.  Income-tax, asset-tax and
mixed-financing scenarios carry it only as a replacement target.

This is not a behavioral tax-revenue forecast or a complete fiscal-balance
effect.  It excludes changes in tax bases, timing, imports, local consumption
tax, other tax receipts, expenditure, interest, macro feedback and general-
government consolidation.  The 5% VAT scenario is deliberately left unquantified
because the 10%/8% rate mix and taxable bases cannot be recovered by simple
linear scaling of aggregate receipts.

For the full-JGB financing scenario, the fiscal reference is also divided by
the official ESRI FY2024 nominal GDP of 642.4147 trillion yen.  The resulting
25.021206715 / 642.4147 = 0.038948683327 ratio, or 3.894868333% of FY2024 nominal
GDP, is reported as a *static incremental JGB-financing share*.  It is not the
change in observed debt/GDP: baseline debt stock, redemptions, other borrowing,
financial assets, nominal-GDP feedback, interest costs and time dynamics are not
modeled.

On the household side, 2024 NSFCW Table 1-21 now supplies an observed
*annual-household-income-decile expenditure diagnostic*. Monthly consumption
expenditure rises from 129,460 yen in annual-income decile 1 to 439,645 yen in
decile 10; the broad food subtotal is 39,658 to 114,333 yen, corresponding to
about 30.63% and 26.01% of consumption at the two endpoints. The source also
provides ten major expenditure groups and household composition. These are
observed descriptive gradients, not VAT-incidence estimates.

The source rank is +HOUSEHOLD_ANNUAL_INCOME_DECILE+, whereas the distributional
objective uses +OECD_NEW_EQUIVALIZED_DISPOSABLE_INCOME_DECILE+. They are not
interchanged. Table 1-21 also reports broad food including alcohol and eating
out, so the food subtotal is not treated as the 8% reduced-rate VAT base. Until
a valid rank bridge and VAT-base/pass-through incidence model exist, income Gini,
FGT2 and real-disposable-income-by-decile policy effects remain empty.

A statutory-rate accounting envelope is additionally reported without assigning
items to VAT classes.  The NTA source states a maximum current consumption-tax
rate of 10% (including the local-consumption-tax component) and a reduced rate
of 8%.  Therefore, for an observed tax-inclusive expenditure amount stem:[E],
the tax content can be bounded mechanically by

[stem]
++++
0 \le T(E) \le E\frac{0.10}{1.10}.
++++

Under a static current basket, unchanged tax-exclusive prices, full pass-through
and no overshifting, the corresponding *maximum* price-relief envelope is
stem:[E(0.10-r')/1.10] for target rate stem:[r'].  Thus the 5% scenario has a
4.5454545% expenditure envelope and the 0%/abolition scenarios a 9.0909091%
envelope.  Annual-income decile 1 has endpoint envelopes of 5,885 yen/month at
5% and 11,770 yen/month at 0%; decile 10 has 19,984 and 39,968 yen/month.

These are deliberately loose accounting envelopes.  Actual expenditure contains
8%-rate and non-taxed/exempt components, the source categories do not identify
the VAT base, and actual pass-through, substitution and quantity responses are
unknown.  The 10% statutory rate includes local consumption tax, whereas the
25.021-trillion-yen fiscal reference above is a central-government cash-receipt
series; the two quantities are not equated.  The envelope is not an estimate of
actual household VAT burden, CPI change, welfare, Gini, FGT2 or disposable-income
effect.

The matrix also contains explicit fields for overall real GDP, annual real
growth, growth-decline penalty, income Gini, wealth Gini, intergenerational gap,
FGT2, real disposable income by decile, inflation, fiscal balance, debt/GDP and
interest-rate/JGB-market effects.

Until the corresponding demand, household-incidence and financing modules are
integrated, their numeric cells remain empty and adjacent status fields identify
the missing link. This is preferable to assigning zero: zero would be an
economic claim, whereas an empty value plus a status is a scientific-state
statement.

== Outputs

* +data/derived/vat_fiscal_receipt_reference.csv+: observed FY2024 central
  tax-receipt metrics.
* +data/derived/vat_policy_fiscal_replacement_reference.csv+: static eight-
  scenario receipt-removal/financing references.
* +data/derived/vat_jgb_debt_gdp_reference.csv+: FY2024 nominal-GDP denominator
  and full-JGB static financing-share reference.
* +data/derived/estat_2024_annual_income_decile_expenditure_diagnostic.csv+:
  observed annual-income-decile household expenditure and composition diagnostic.
* +data/derived/vat_household_tax_content_envelope.csv+: eight scenarios x ten
  annual-income deciles of statutory-rate tax-content/price-relief envelopes.
* +data/derived/vat_policy_scenario_matrix.csv+: 8 scenarios x 300
  institutional stress points = 2,400 rows.
* +data/derived/vat_policy_scenario_summary.csv+: one row per policy scenario
  with the institutional range and joint outcome-status vector.

== Scientific status

----
PARTIAL_E2E_POLICY_MATRIX_WITH_STATIC_FISCAL_AND_HOUSEHOLD_RATE_ENVELOPES
----

The matrix is operational end-to-end, but it is not yet a policy forecast or a
joint welfare optimization. The next completion step is to build a defensible bridge from observed household
expenditure to the objective-rank distribution and VAT-incidence channels, or to
advance another currently explicit unresolved channel with official inputs.

= VAT policy scenario integration matrix
:revdate: 2026-09-11

== Purpose

This adapter satisfies the integration step of Issue #26 without inventing
unidentified macro effects. It maps the existing VAT institutional
compliance/allocation stress module into all eight requested policy scenarios
and reports GDP, growth, distribution, poverty, inflation, fiscal and debt
outcome statuses in the same rows.

It is deliberately an *integration layer*, not a replacement macro model.

== Numeric versus unresolved outputs

The adapter numerically carries:

* the model-contingent institutional GDP level component
  stem:[m(\rho c_{VAT}+a_{alloc})];
* its annualized transition-growth contribution; and
* a static FY2024 central-government VAT cash-receipt replacement reference.
The stress range remains *not an empirical bound*. Current evidence does not
identify national stem:[c_{VAT}], stem:[\rho], or stem:[a_{alloc}].

The fiscal reference is parsed from the Ministry of Finance FY2024 treasury
revenue table.  Consumption-tax cash receipts are 25,021,206,715 thousand yen,
or 25.021206715 trillion yen.  Total tax receipts are 74.187865765 trillion yen,
so consumption tax represents about 33.73% of the recorded central tax receipts.
For the 0% and full-abolition scenarios this amount is carried as a *static gross
receipt-removal/replacement reference*.  For the JGB scenario the same amount is
shown as a one-for-one gross JGB financing reference.  Income-tax, asset-tax and
mixed-financing scenarios carry it only as a replacement target.

This is not a behavioral tax-revenue forecast or a complete fiscal-balance
effect.  It excludes changes in tax bases, timing, imports, local consumption
tax, other tax receipts, expenditure, interest, macro feedback and general-
government consolidation.  The 5% VAT scenario is deliberately left unquantified
because the 10%/8% rate mix and taxable bases cannot be recovered by simple
linear scaling of aggregate receipts.

For the full-JGB financing scenario, the fiscal reference is also divided by
the official ESRI FY2024 nominal GDP of 642.4147 trillion yen.  The resulting
25.021206715 / 642.4147 = 0.038948683327 ratio, or 3.894868333% of FY2024 nominal
GDP, is reported as a *static incremental JGB-financing share*.  It is not the
change in observed debt/GDP: baseline debt stock, redemptions, other borrowing,
financial assets, nominal-GDP feedback, interest costs and time dynamics are not
modeled by that static benchmark.

== Dynamic incremental fiscal/JGB accounting

Issue #56 adds a separate stock-flow-consistent *incremental policy-debt*
sensitivity module.  It does not reconstruct or forecast Japan's total government
debt stock.  The opening incremental debt is zero and the FY2024 central VAT cash-
receipt reference of 25.021206715 trillion yen is repeated as a constant nominal
annual gap only for the financing sensitivity.  The accounting identity is

[stem]
++++
D_t = D_{t-1} - R_t + I_t^{gross},\qquad
I_t^{gross}=R_t+G_t+iD_{t-1}+\Delta A_t,
++++

where stem:[G_t] is the policy primary gap financed with JGBs, stem:[R_t]
redemptions, stem:[i] the exogenous effective interest-rate sensitivity and
stem:[\Delta A_t] the financial-asset change (fixed at zero in this first model).
Redemptions are shown explicitly and refinanced, so they affect gross issuance but
do not by themselves change net incremental debt.

The committed sensitivity grid crosses effective interest rates of 1%, 2% and 4%
with nominal-GDP growth of 0%, 2% and 4%, uses a 20% annual redemption/refinancing
rate, a ten-year horizon, and a 50% primary JGB share for the mixed-financing case.
These are versioned *model sensitivities, not empirical bounds*.  The stored annual
paths use the central +r02_g02+ case; the summary reports extrema over all nine
rate-growth combinations.

Under those accounting assumptions, year-10 incremental debt/GDP is 27.53--46.76%
for full JGB financing and 13.76--23.38% for the 50%-JGB mixed case. Income-tax and
asset-tax replacement show zero incremental policy debt only *conditional on full
replacement of the static target*.  The results are not total government debt/GDP,
fiscal-sustainability estimates, interest-rate forecasts, or endogenous JGB-market
responses.  VAT demand and institutional productivity effects are not inserted into
nominal GDP here, which prevents double counting with the separate policy modules.

On the household side, 2024 NSFCW Table 1-21 now supplies an observed
*annual-household-income-decile expenditure diagnostic*. Monthly consumption
expenditure rises from 129,460 yen in annual-income decile 1 to 439,645 yen in
decile 10; the broad food subtotal is 39,658 to 114,333 yen, corresponding to
about 30.63% and 26.01% of consumption at the two endpoints. The source also
provides ten major expenditure groups and household composition. These are
observed descriptive gradients, not VAT-incidence estimates.

The source rank is +HOUSEHOLD_ANNUAL_INCOME_DECILE+, whereas the distributional
objective uses +OECD_NEW_EQUIVALIZED_DISPOSABLE_INCOME_DECILE+. They are not
interchanged. Table 1-21 also reports broad food including alcohol and eating
out, so the food subtotal is not treated as the 8% reduced-rate VAT base. Until
a valid rank bridge and VAT-base/pass-through incidence model exist, income Gini,
FGT2 and real-disposable-income-by-decile policy effects remain empty.

A statutory-rate accounting envelope is additionally reported without assigning
items to VAT classes.  The NTA source states a maximum current consumption-tax
rate of 10% (including the local-consumption-tax component) and a reduced rate
of 8%.  Therefore, for an observed tax-inclusive expenditure amount stem:[E],
the tax content can be bounded mechanically by

[stem]
++++
0 \le T(E) \le E\frac{0.10}{1.10}.
++++

Under a static current basket, unchanged tax-exclusive prices, full pass-through
and no overshifting, the corresponding *maximum* price-relief envelope is
stem:[E(0.10-r')/1.10] for target rate stem:[r'].  Thus the 5% scenario has a
4.5454545% expenditure envelope and the 0%/abolition scenarios a 9.0909091%
envelope.  Annual-income decile 1 has endpoint envelopes of 5,885 yen/month at
5% and 11,770 yen/month at 0%; decile 10 has 19,984 and 39,968 yen/month.

These are deliberately loose accounting envelopes.  Actual expenditure contains
8%-rate and non-taxed/exempt components, the source categories do not identify
the VAT base, and actual pass-through, substitution and quantity responses are
unknown.  The 10% statutory rate includes local consumption tax, whereas the
25.021-trillion-yen fiscal reference above is a central-government cash-receipt
series; the two quantities are not equated.  The envelope is not an estimate of
actual household VAT burden, CPI change, welfare, Gini, FGT2 or disposable-income
effect.

=== Historical pass-through and quantity evidence

+data/derived/vat_pass_through_evidence.csv+ keeps Japan historical evidence,
mechanical assumptions, statutory scope and external temporary-cut evidence
separate. Shiraishi's 2014 POS evidence finds heterogeneous retail pass-through,
including under- and over-shifting. The Bank of Japan's July 2019 full-pass-through
input is retained as a *mechanical CPI assumption*, not relabeled as an empirical
estimate. The same BOJ outlook separates front-loading/subsequent decline from
the real-income channel. The Cabinet Office's ex-post 2014 estimate places the
front-loading/rebound timing shift at 2.5--3.3 trillion yen (about 0.5--0.6% of
real GDP), but this is intertemporal substitution around a tax increase, not a
steady-state VAT-cut demand elasticity. Ministry of Finance material fixes the
10%/8% statutory scope, while decile-specific standard/reduced/exempt shares are
still unidentified.

A bounded Issue #89 search adds two direct *external* temporary-cut episodes.
Germany's 2020 temporary VAT cut produced about 70% supermarket price pass-through
in a Germany-versus-Austria design; the subsequent rate restoration had only about
half the price effect in the cited study scope. The United Kingdom's 2008 temporary
2.5-percentage-point cut initially lowered prices, but part of pass-through was
reversed within months; retail-sales volume rose around 1% and then fell after the
cut ended, consistent with substantial intertemporal substitution. These results
show why increase-to-decrease symmetry and a time-invariant pass-through parameter
should not be imposed mechanically.

The official Japanese standard-rate history used in the same audit enumerates the
1989 introduction at 3% and increases to 5%, 8%, and 10%, but no implemented
nationwide standard-rate decrease episode. Consequently the German 0.70 estimate
and the UK temporary response are *external sensitivities*, not a formal bound on
Japan. +data/derived/vat_rate_cut_transport_audit.csv+ records the stop rule. The
Japan 5%, 0%, and abolition pass-through parameter remains +NOT_IDENTIFIED+, and
full institutional abolition remains distinct from a temporary statutory-rate cut.

=== Annual-income-decile VAT rate-scope proxy

The same NSFCW Table 1-21 provides separate annual-income-decile rows for food,
alcohol, dining out and newspapers.  Combining those survey categories with the
MOF reduced-rate description yields a deliberately limited rate-scope diagnostic:
+food - alcohol - dining out+ is the core food-category proxy, while adding all
newspaper expenditure gives a diagnostic ceiling for survey-category coverage.
At decile 1 these amounts are 33,195 and 34,616 yen/month; at decile 10 they are
80,594 and 82,537 yen/month.

These figures are *not* transaction-level VAT bases.  The survey taxonomy is not
a legal transaction crosswalk, and the newspaper row does not identify which
purchases satisfy the qualifying subscription rule.  Seller/taxability status is
also not observed.  The matrix therefore carries the proxy values together with
+TRANSACTION_LEVEL_VAT_BASE_NOT_IDENTIFIED+ status rather than using them to
calibrate a tax-incidence or pass-through parameter.

Accordingly, the policy matrix exposes a blank +policy_pass_through_parameter+
for every non-baseline scenario.  Historical rate-increase evidence is not
extrapolated into a point estimate for a 5%, 0%, or institutional-abolition
scenario.  Gini, FGT2 and real-disposable-income effects remain blank because the
objective-rank bridge and household VAT-base/incidence mapping are still missing.

=== 2024 restricted objective-rank route

Issue #86 now has a concrete restricted-data route rather than another public-table
search.  The e-Micro onsite catalog lists the 2024 NSFCW, and its official
secondary metadata expose three 2024 data systems.  Two are relevant here:
+M4AR8-DS51+ (income/assets aggregation) and +M4AR8-DS52+ (detailed two-month-
average household accounts).

DS51 exposes household annual income (+I_Bdy001+), annual disposable income on
the OECD-new basis (+I_Bdy031+), household size and population weights.  DS52
exposes household annual income, detailed consumption expenditure, food, alcohol,
dining-out, newspaper expenditure and population weights.  Both metadata files
also expose +SetaiFugo+ and +Ichiren+ fields.  However, the separate +SetaiID+
field is documented as empty/not used for 2024 aggregation, and the metadata do
*not* by themselves establish the complete authorized cross-system join key.
Additional geography/unit fields may be part of the required composite key.  The
DS51-to-DS52 same-household join is therefore a provider-confirmation gate, not
an assumed fact.

If the provider confirms that cross-system household join inside the approved
onsite environment, the route can directly construct the missing household-unit
joint bridge between objective-rank income inputs and detailed expenditure.  It
would still not by itself identify legal transaction-level VAT bases or policy
pass-through.  If the join is not permitted/stable, DS51 alone lacks detailed
consumption and DS52 alone lacks the exact DS51 OECD-new annual-disposable-income
input, so neither is silently promoted to the objective rank.

The current National Statistics Center custom-tabulation listing names 2019 and
older NSFCW years, not 2024.  A 2024 custom table is therefore a provider-inquiry
fallback only; it is not treated as currently available.  Broad public aggregate
search remains stopped under the existing rule.  Until approved data access and
the join gate are resolved, objective-rank household VAT incidence, Gini, FGT2
and real-disposable-income effects remain +NOT_IDENTIFIED+.

The machine-readable contracts are:

* +data/derived/estat_objective_rank_restricted_route_audit_2024.csv+; and
* +data/derived/estat_objective_rank_restricted_variable_map_2024.csv+.

Raw questionnaire microdata cannot be committed to this repository.  Reproduction
for this route is therefore code/specification plus provider-reviewed,
disclosure-safe outputs.

The matrix also contains explicit fields for overall real GDP, annual real
growth, growth-decline penalty, income Gini, wealth Gini, intergenerational gap,
FGT2, real disposable income by decile, inflation, fiscal balance, debt/GDP and
interest-rate/JGB-market effects.

Until the corresponding demand, household-incidence and financing modules are
integrated, their numeric cells remain empty and adjacent status fields identify
the missing link. This is preferable to assigning zero: zero would be an
economic claim, whereas an empty value plus a status is a scientific-state
statement.

== Pareto screening rule

+pareto_objectives.csv+ freezes six core headline objectives before any scalar welfare
weights are used: discounted cumulative real growth, growth-decline penalty, FGT2,
income Gini, wealth Gini and intergenerational inequality. Inflation, debt service,
JGB-market function and exchange-rate conditions are constraint families rather than
Pareto welfare objectives; overall GDP level and related diagnostics remain dashboard
metrics. The general contract is +data/policy_evaluation_contract.csv+.

Robust dominance is intentionally strict: for every required objective, both scenarios
must have numerical lower/upper bounds; one scenario must be no worse over the full
interval on every objective and strictly better on at least one. If any required
objective is unbounded or unidentified, the pair is
+INDETERMINATE_MISSING_REQUIRED_OBJECTIVES+ rather than being treated as tied or
non-dominated.

The current repository therefore does not claim an optimal scenario. None of the six
core Pareto outcomes currently has a policy-usable numerical bound for the eight VAT
scenarios, so all scenarios are +UNRESOLVED_NOT_ENOUGH_IDENTIFIED_OBJECTIVES+. The
existing incremental-debt/JGB paths remain model-contingent constraint diagnostics,
not a welfare objective. No normative welfare weights are used by this screen; any
later single-policy rule must be explicitly normative and applied only after the
identification gate and robust Pareto layer.

joint welfare optimization.  M2 public-data work is decision-ready under explicit
identification limits: the objective-rank public-table search and general
rate-cut pass-through search are stopped, the dynamic fiscal/JGB sensitivity is
implemented, and the Pareto layer refuses to rank scenarios whose required
objectives are not numerically bounded.  The next identification step is the

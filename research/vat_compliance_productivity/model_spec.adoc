= VAT abolition compliance-productivity channel
:toc:
:stem: latexmath
:revdate: 2026-09-10

== Purpose

This module separates the institutional productivity effect of removing
VAT-specific administration from the ordinary demand effect of changing the
statutory VAT rate.

It is deliberately narrower than the repository's eventual macro-distribution
model.

The Phase-1 question is:

----
Can 0% VAT with the administrative system retained be represented as a
different policy state from complete VAT abolition, without pretending that
the resulting productivity dividend is empirically point-identified?
----

Yes.

== Policy-state separation

The regime file contains four mutually exclusive states:

[cols="3,1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Regime | Stylized VAT rate | VAT administration | Invoice system
| Current 8/10 | 10% / 8% | retained | retained
| Reduced 5 | 5% | retained | retained
| Zero rate | 0% | retained | retained
| Full abolition | 0% | abolished | abolished
|===

The rate vector is therefore insufficient to define the policy.

Let stem:[s_{VAT}] be the VAT administrative state and let
stem:[a(s_{VAT})] be the fraction of VAT-specific administrative obligations
removed.

For the current Phase-1 scenarios:

[stem]
++++
a(s_{VAT}) =
\begin{cases}
0,& \text{administration retained},\\
1,& \text{institutional abolition}.
\end{cases}
++++

This implies mechanically that:

----
0% VAT + administration retained != full institutional abolition
----

== Observed evidence and non-identified parameters

Observed evidence is stored separately from model assumptions.

The evidence registry establishes, among other things:

* high reported incidence of invoice-related administrative burden;
* persistent reported non-tax cost increases;
* concentration of bookkeeping responsibility in very small firms;
* METI's FY2021 survey directly reports internal tax-procedure hours for
  consumption tax in a respondent subset (n=1,514), while Q8-3 does not
  explicitly label the tax-item time measure as annual;
* a broader all-tax compliance-cost burden that is relatively larger for SMEs;
* empirical VAT-threshold bunching and evidence that compliance costs matter
  for small-enterprise responses; and
* current institutional filing, input-credit, invoice-retention, and exemption
  threshold mechanisms.

=== METI VAT-specific internal-hours bound

The corporate survey covered a nationwide target of 20,000 corporations drawn
from the Teikoku Databank business database under stated industry conditions,
and reports 4,410 valid responses (22.1%).  The consumption-tax hours item has
a smaller published n=1,514.

METI publishes nine one-decimal percentage bins from 0 hours through an
open-ended 100-hours-or-more category.  Enforcing n=1,514 and treating each
published percentage as rounded to the nearest 0.1 percentage point leaves 28
jointly feasible integer count vectors.  Using only each bin's lower endpoint
gives the conditional respondent-subset mean bound:

----
mean VAT-specific internal hours >= 15.126155878468 hours/respondent (reported Q8-3 period)
----

The 100+ category prevents any finite empirical upper bound.  If an additional
top-code cap is imposed only as a stress assumption, the corresponding upper
suprema are 24.0350 hours at a 100-hour cap, 27.4036 at 150 hours, 34.1407 at
250 hours, 50.9835 at 500 hours, and 84.6691 at 1,000 hours.  These are not
confidence limits or population upper bounds.

The published source also requires a sample-scope caution.  Results p.66 says
the hours item targets respondents answering ``yes'' to the preceding Q8-1,
whereas questionnaire Q8-3 explicitly targets Q8-1 ``No'' respondents.  The
results-page label is also numerically impossible as a literal selector: p.65
reports n=3,965 and 17.0% yes, so one-decimal rounding permits only 673--676 yes
responses, fewer than the VAT item n=1,514.  The repository preserves this
source conflict rather than inventing a sample definition.

METI p.70 also reports annual external tax-procedure outsourcing expenditure,
but it is not disaggregated by tax type.  It therefore cannot be assigned to
VAT.

Consequently, the METI result partially identifies VAT-specific *internal
hours within the observed respondent subset and reported Q8-3 period*.  Because
the tax-item hours are not explicitly labelled annual, it cannot yet be
multiplied by an annual wage benchmark as though its period were identified.
It also does not identify a Japan-wide VAT-only real-resource share of baseline
output.

=== BSWS wage-conversion bridge

RIETI 21-P-018 states that tax-procedure labor time was valued using
industry-specific hourly wages from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare
Basic Survey on Wage Structure.  The paper does not identify the exact e-Stat
table or the exact arithmetic used to construct its hourly wage, so this
repository does not claim an exact replication of that unpublished step.

For 2019 e-Stat Table 1 (statInfId 000031919751; DB sid 0003084009), the
repository preserves four official components for 17 industry-total/major-
industry rows: scheduled actual hours, overtime actual hours, regular cash
earnings, and scheduled cash earnings.  Two transparent wage candidates are
then defined:

[stem]
++++
w^{sched}_j = rac{1000\,S_j}{H^{sched}_j},
\qquad
w^{eff}_j = rac{1000\,R_j}{H^{sched}_j+H^{ot}_j},
++++

where stem:[S_j] is scheduled cash earnings in thousand yen, stem:[R_j] is
regular cash earnings in thousand yen, and the two hour variables are monthly
actual hours.  For the industry-total row these yield 1,923.125 yen/hour and
1,953.757225433526 yen/hour.  Across the 16 major-industry rows the transparent
rate range is approximately 1,466--2,852 yen/hour depending on formula and
industry.

Combining either hourly rate with the METI conditional lower-bound hours is a
*mechanical cross-source conversion*, not a newly identified annual cost.  At
the industry-total rates:

----
15.126155878468 h x 1,923.125 yen/h = about 29,089 yen/respondent/Q8-3 reported period
15.126155878468 h x 1,953.757225... yen/h = about 29,553 yen/respondent/Q8-3 reported period
----

The full 34-scenario industry/formula table is retained for sensitivity.  The
conversion still lacks an explicitly annual Q8-3 period, respondent-subset
industry weights, population-selection correction, temporal alignment between
2019 wages and the FY2021 survey, and VAT-specific external outsourcing.
Therefore it cannot be interpreted as Japan-wide stem:[c_{VAT}].

Therefore:

----
c_VAT  = NOT_IDENTIFIED
rho    = NOT_IDENTIFIED
a_alloc= NOT_IDENTIFIED
----

where:

* stem:[c_{VAT}] is the counterfactual real-resource share tied specifically to
  VAT administration;
* stem:[\rho] is the productive redeployment fraction; and
* stem:[a_{alloc}] is the abolition-only allocative-efficiency level effect.

The numerical grids are stress tests, not confidence intervals, identified
sets, estimates, or empirical bounds.
== Compliance-productivity channel

For a regime with administrative-removal fraction stem:[m], define released
VAT administrative resources as:

[stem]
++++
R_{VAT}=m c_{VAT}.
++++

Only a fraction stem:[\rho] is assumed to become measured productive output:

[stem]
++++
\Delta y_{compliance}=\rho R_{VAT}
=m\rho c_{VAT}.
++++

This explicitly prevents the forbidden one-for-one rule:

[stem]
++++
\Delta y_{compliance}=c_{VAT}
++++

unless a stress-test point *separately* sets both stem:[m=1] and
stem:[\rho=1].

The allocative channel is kept separate:

[stem]
++++
\Delta y_{allocation}=m a_{alloc}.
++++

The institutional output-level sensitivity is therefore:

[stem]
++++
\Delta y_{institution}
=m(\rho c_{VAT}+a_{alloc}).
++++

The module does not use the VAT rate in this equation.

That is intentional: rate effects belong to the demand/tax wedge channel, not
to the abolition-of-administration channel.

== Transition-growth reporting

If a level effect is mechanically spread over stem:[T] years, the module
reports the annualized transition contribution:

[stem]
++++
g_{institution,T}
=
(1+\Delta y_{institution})^{1/T}-1.
++++

This is *not* a forecast of annual real GDP growth.

It is only the annualized contribution of the model-contingent level effect.
The repository objective stem:[g_t>0] still requires the full macro model.

----
STRESS_TEST_NOT_EMPIRICAL_BOUND
----

The four regimes times 300 parameter combinations produce 1,200 rows.
== End-to-end Phase-1 result

All three administration-retained regimes satisfy, for every stress point:

[stem]
++++
\Delta y_{institution}=0.
++++

This includes the 0% VAT regime.

The full-abolition regime spans:

----
minimum institutional level effect = 0
maximum institutional level effect = 0.006
----

The 0.006 value is the maximum of the chosen stress grid:

[stem]
++++
1\times(1\times0.005+0.001)=0.006.
++++

It is not an empirical upper bound.

The result is useful because it proves the architecture:

----
rate reduction channel     : separate
administration abolition   : separate
compliance resource release: separate
allocative efficiency      : separate
financing                  : separate
distribution / FGT2        : not yet linked
----

Every sensitivity row records:

----
ordinary_vat_demand_effect_status = EXCLUDED_FROM_COMPLIANCE_MODULE
income_gini_effect_status         = NOT_MODELED_PHASE1
wealth_gini_effect_status         = NOT_MODELED_PHASE1

fiscal_balance_effect_status      = FINANCING_CHANNEL_SEPARATE
debt_gdp_effect_status            = FINANCING_CHANNEL_SEPARATE
----

This prevents a firm-side administrative-resource model from silently becoming
a full welfare model.

== Phase-2 requirements

Issue #26 remains open after this Phase-1 implementation.

Before policy optimization, additional work is required to identify or bound:

* an identified reporting period and population weights for VAT-specific hours;
* VAT-specific monetary resource costs by firm size and industry;
* the exact RIETI BSWS hourly-wage construction, or a justified replacement;
* the fraction of saved owner/worker time that becomes productive activity;
* persistent versus transitional software/adviser costs;
* allocative effects from exemption-threshold and invoice-network distortions;
* household incidence by income/wealth/age group;
* demand and pass-through effects;
* revenue and financing effects under JGB, income-tax, asset-tax and mixed
  packages; and
* inflation, debt/GDP, interest-rate and JGB-market feedbacks.

Only after those links exist can the repository jointly report GDP, growth,
Gini, FGT2, fiscal and debt outcomes for Issue #26.

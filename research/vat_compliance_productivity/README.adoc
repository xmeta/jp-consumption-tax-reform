Phase 2 now has a directly observed METI VAT-specific internal-hours
distribution for n=1,514 respondents, nested within a nationwide corporation
survey with 20,000 targets and 4,410 valid responses.  Rounding-consistent partial
identification gives a 15.126155878468 hours/respondent lower bound for the reported Q8-3 period, while
the 100+ top code leaves the uncapped upper bound open.  Q8-3 does not explicitly
label the tax-item hours as annual.  This is not a national firm mean and does
not identify +c_VAT+.

The 2019--2021 survey-lineage audit closes an important interpretation gap.
The 2019 corporate Q6-1 questionnaire explicitly scopes VAT hours to a target
fiscal year, but its public results do not publish the VAT-hour responses and
one process column includes accounting-purpose work.  The 2020 corporate Q11-3
questionnaire collects VAT-specific post-financial-statement hours, and RIETI
21-P-018 says the underlying survey answers concern FY2019 corporate behavior,
but the METI public report does not publish Q11-3 values and the official
publication listing has no attached data file.  The 2021 survey is the opposite:
it publishes an n=1,514 VAT-hours distribution but leaves the tax-item period
non-explicit and has conflicting selector metadata.  No public year therefore
jointly supplies an explicit fiscal period, numeric VAT hours, and unambiguous
selection.  Historical designs must not be used to annualize the 2021 bound.  The archived
FY2021 METI publication list also shows no data attachment for report 000139,
so the public publication channel does not provide the missing microdata.

A separate transition-cost layer now uses SMRJ FY2019 program-performance data
for the consumption-tax reduced-rate support program.  The program subsidized
part of eligible expenses for multi-rate registers, ordering-system
modifications, invoice-management-system modifications, and related
implementation.  FY2019 records show 94,875 grants totaling 22,914,612,620 yen;
cumulative records through FY2019 show 174,781 grants totaling 44,659,886,587
yen.  Because the subsidy covers only part of eligible expense, these flows
imply selected grant-record transition-expenditure lower bounds of about
241,524 yen/grant in FY2019 and 255,519 yen/grant cumulatively.  These are not
unique-firm means, population estimates, recurring annual costs, or additions
to +c_VAT+.

Historical threshold-distortion evidence is now quantitative rather than purely
qualitative.  RIETI 21-E-090 uses Japanese Census of Manufacture data and VAT
reforms to estimate convergence-method marginal-buncher sales-response upper
bounds.  For the three 30-million-yen threshold periods used in the structural
estimation, the published +Delta y / y*+ inputs are 55.0%, 54.3%, and 54.2%.
The same study estimates its broad compliance-cost parameter +theta+, defined
as compliance cost relative to value added, at 0.130--0.140 for the all-enterprise
sample, 0.091--0.111 for firms, and 0.116--0.130 for sole proprietors across the
1992/1997 reform comparisons.  These are local historical structural estimates,
not a national GDP-loss rate.  The paper's cost concept includes monetary, time,
operational, and psychological burden; its model assumes no VAT pass-through and
omits the simplified tax system near the threshold.  Therefore neither +theta+
nor the marginal-buncher response is used directly as +c_VAT+ or +a_alloc+.
The current 10-million-yen, invoice-era aggregate allocation effect remains
+NOT_IDENTIFIED+.

A second threshold study moves the evidence closer to the present exemption
threshold.  Ichikawa, Arudchelvan and Onji (2019) study the 10-million-yen
threshold using Teikoku Databank data and nationalize selected quantities with
the 2016 Economic Census.  They estimate 56,651 excess-bunching firms and
16.85 billion yen/year of tax windfall.  Their Table 4 gives two sharply
different *mechanism-sensitive* lost-sales calculations: 84.98 billion yen if
all excess bunchers are assumed to make a 1.5-million-yen real sales adjustment,
versus 3.14 billion yen using the observed missing mass with the same per-firm
adjustment assumption.  The more-than-27-fold gap is not interpreted as a formal
identification interval.  The paper itself treats the real-adjustment versus
tax-avoidance mechanism as unresolved; lost sales are also not one-for-one GDP
or welfare loss because displaced sales may be supplied by competitors.

The same study uses the 2014 three-percentage-point VAT-rate increase as a
quasi-experimental comparison.  For all four same-window specifications, the
pre/post 95% confidence intervals overlap under the paper's stated decision
rule, so it does not detect an increase in bunching despite the larger monetary
benefit of exemption.  This supports a compliance-burden motive but does not
prove it.  Crucially, the study predates the October 2023 qualified-invoice
system.  The current invoice-era aggregate allocation effect and macro
+a_alloc+ therefore remain +NOT_IDENTIFIED+.

Post-2023 invoice-era evidence is now quantitative at the transaction-network
incidence level.  JCCI 2024 reports that, among n=782 respondents that had
purchased from exempt businesses before invoice introduction, 74.0% continued
nearly all such transactions, while 7.9% continued only some, 12.8% continued
only very few, and 5.2% ended all.  The explicit contraction categories therefore
sum to 25.9% (subject to published rounding).  JCCI 2025 reports that 43.7% of
n=1,151 general-taxation invoice registrants still purchase from exempt
businesses; among n=502 such respondents, 42.3% state that they plan to review
prices or suppliers, including 11.8% that plan to stop all or most such
purchases.  Among n=92 respondents planning to continue at unchanged prices,
44.6% cite no alternative supplier, 39.1% cite search effort that is not worth
the cost, and 38.0% cite labor-shortage/capacity needs.

These figures establish *post-2023 network adjustment and substitution-friction
incidence*.  They do not identify transaction-value loss, a causal counterfactual,
productivity loss, or a population-weighted GDP effect.  Future plans are not
realized behavior, multiple-response shares are not mutually exclusive firm
fractions, and the 2024/2025 respondents are not the same businesses.  Therefore
no simple cross-year registration trend is inferred and macro +a_alloc+ remains
+NOT_IDENTIFIED+.

The official SME Agency FY2025 invoice-transaction survey adds a larger, more
direct realized-transaction layer.  Its TSR frame targeted 50,000 small
businesses and received 15,425 responses (30.9%).  For the largest customer
relationship, 3.8% of n=14,732 report that the transaction price was reduced or
the transaction stopped after invoice introduction; the published one-decimal
share is compatible with 553--567 responses.  Separately, 14.3% of n=15,321
report that a customer required invoice registration as a condition for
continuing transactions (2,184--2,198 compatible responses), and 12.8% of
n=14,690 report that no price-negotiation opportunity was provided
(1,873--1,887 compatible responses).  The adverse price/stop share ranges from
3.2% to 7.9% across the seven published industry groups, with transport/postal
highest at 7.9%.

This materially strengthens the post-2023 evidence because the price/stop item
is a reported realized outcome rather than a future intention.  It still does
not identify the yen value of the loss, separate price reduction from complete
transaction stop, establish the counterfactual without the invoice system, or
provide population weights.  The report also compares 3.6% in December 2023
with 3.8% in July 2025, but these are different cross-sectional waves and are
not treated as a panel or causal trend.  Macro +a_alloc+ therefore remains
+NOT_IDENTIFIED+.

The wage bridge adds 2019 MHLW Basic Survey on Wage Structure components for
17 industry-total/major-industry rows. Two transparent hourly-wage formulas are
kept because RIETI 21-P-018 names the survey and industry-hourly-wage concept
but does not identify the exact table/formula it used. At the industry-total
benchmarks, mechanically valuing the METI lower-bound hours gives about
29.1--29.6 thousand yen per respondent for the reported Q8-3 period. This is
not an annual, population, GDP-share, or national +c_VAT+ estimate.

== Scientific status

MODEL_CONTINGENT_COMPLIANCE_PRODUCTIVITY_SENSITIVITY_PHASE1_COMPLETE
----

This module does not identify the optimal VAT rate and does not supply a
macro GDP forecast, Gini effect, FGT2 effect, fiscal effect or debt path.

The compliance-only outputs are now consumed by
+research/vat_policy_integration/run_vat_policy_scenario_matrix.py+, which maps
them into the eight Issue #26 policy scenarios.  That adapter jointly reports
the status of GDP, growth, Gini, FGT2, inflation, fiscal and debt outcomes while
leaving unavailable macro channels explicitly unmodeled rather than assigning
zero.  This is a partial end-to-end integration, not a policy forecast.

The ordinary VAT demand channel and financing choices remain separate.

= Private-salary taxpayer-status transition diagnostic
:toc:
:stem: latexmath

== Status

----
EXTERNAL_PAYROLL_TAX_DIAGNOSTIC_NO_CROSS_SOURCE_UNION
----

This audit asks whether the 2024 National Tax Agency Private Salary Survey can
sharpen the missing calculated-tax-positive denominator identified in
`calculated_tax_positive_denominator_audit.adoc`.

It adds an external wage-side diagnostic. It does not alter v1-v5 model
results or Paper 1 claims.

== Official sources

The repository retains:

* `NTA-MINKAN-2024-T16`: 2024 Private Salary Survey Table 16,
  salary-class taxpayer/non-taxpayer counts;
* `NTA-MINKAN-METHODOLOGY`: the official methodology and terminology page.

The methodology states that the survey target includes private-sector salary
earners regardless of whether income tax is payable. It defines a
`納税者` as a salary earner for whom source-withheld income tax exists.

It also defines `乙欄適用者` as an independently counted secondary-payroll
record when one salary earner receives salary from two or more payers. This is
why Table 16 is reproduced both including and excluding those records.

== Table 16 structure

The workbook has four panels:

. full-year salary earners;
. less-than-year salary earners;
. full-year salary earners excluding secondary-payroll (乙欄) records;
. less-than-year salary earners excluding secondary-payroll records.

Each panel contains fourteen salary classes and separately reports:

* year-end-adjusted taxpayers, non-taxpayers and total;
* not-year-end-adjusted taxpayers, non-taxpayers and total;
* all taxpayers, non-taxpayers and total;
* corresponding tax amounts.

The class-level extraction retains all 56 panel-by-salary-class rows rather
than selecting a preferred salary range.

== Why this is not the missing final calculated-tax denominator

The Table 16 taxpayer concept is source-withholding based.

Year-end adjustment makes the year-end-adjusted subgroup much more informative
than a raw monthly withholding indicator, because payroll tax has been
annualized using payroll information available to the employer.

However, a later final income-tax return can introduce deductions, credits,
loss offsets, or other information that changes final calculated tax and the
self-assessed balance.

Therefore:

----
35,556,416 is NOT asserted to be a strict lower bound on final
calculated-tax-positive persons.
----

It is an external scale diagnostic for positive payroll income tax after
year-end adjustment.

The not-year-end-adjusted taxpayer count is weaker still: positive source
withholding can later be refunded or offset.

== Cross-source overlap problem

The self-assessed-return audit identifies 5,158,260 persons with positive
self-assessed balance.

It is tempting to form

[stem]
++++
35{,}556{,}416 + 5{,}158{,}260
=
40{,}714{,}676.
++++

That addition is not identified.

A person represented in the Private Salary Survey may also file a final
return and enter the self-assessed-return population. The public tables used
here do not provide the cross-source overlap.

Consequently the repository marks the union status as:

----
OVERLAP_WITH_SELF_ASSESSED_RETURNS_UNKNOWN_DO_NOT_SUM
----

No union count is reported or used for calibration.

== Rounding audit

Table 16 contains published integer estimates. Across the 56 salary-class
rows, reproduced arithmetic shows small displayed-value rounding residuals:

* taxpayer + non-taxpayer versus total: at most 1 person within a class;
* salary-class sums versus published panel totals: at most 2 persons;
* salary-class tax sums versus published panel totals: at most 2 million yen.

The extractor treats only residuals beyond these observed publication-rounding
limits as failures.

== Identification consequence

The Private Salary Survey materially improves scale knowledge:

* the positive-payroll-tax population is tens of millions, not approximately
  five million;
* the five-million self-assessed positive-balance target therefore cannot be
  interpreted as the universe of persons bearing positive income tax;
* payroll and self-assessed-return statistics cover overlapping but different
  administrative/event concepts.

But the new source does not identify:

* final calculated-tax-positive persons across all settlement channels;
* overlap between payroll-tax-positive persons and self-assessed filers;
* the transition from year-end-adjusted positive payroll tax to final
  calculated tax after a return;
* person-level rank correspondence to F71561.

Therefore this evidence remains an external diagnostic and is not used to
calibrate the pseudo calculated-tax-positive rate, v5 stem:[eta], or a
cross-source union count.

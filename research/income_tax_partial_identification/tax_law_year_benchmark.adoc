= 2024 tax-law 2x2 benchmark

== Status

`SENSITIVITY_ONLY_NOT_IDENTIFIED_TAX_LAW_YEAR_2X2_BENCHMARK`.

Issue 133 uses the same 2024 household and administrative aggregates and the
same frozen v2--v5 bridge specifications throughout.  The benchmark varies two
model inputs separately: the statutory tax-law year used to evaluate pseudo tax
units, and the statutory tax-law year under which each decile/scenario nuisance
income scale was calibrated.

== Reproducible statutory inputs

The 2024 law set is generated from repository-tracked National Tax Agency 2024
filing-guide pages covering employment income, public-pension miscellaneous
income, the basic deduction, dependent deductions, and the ordinary progressive
income-tax quick table.  Each raw source is registered in
`data/source_catalog.csv` with its locator and SHA-256; the normalized rules are
`data/derived/income_tax_2024_statutory_parameters.csv`.

The Table-17 dependent-composition sensitivity now applies the dependent-
deduction amounts from the selected statutory-law module.  The ordinary 2026
production path retains its pre-existing values exactly; the 2024 benchmark no
longer obtains its statutory amounts from the 2026-derived proxy column.

== 2x2 design

`run_tax_law_year_benchmark.py` first calibrates the nuisance scales under 2024
law and under 2026 law.  It then evaluates both statutory-law sets under each
fixed scale map.  The four cells are therefore:

* 2024 law / 2024-law calibration: same-year recalibrated benchmark;
* 2026 law / 2024-law calibration: law substitution holding the 2024 scale fixed;
* 2024 law / 2026-law calibration: reverse law substitution holding the 2026 scale fixed;
* 2026 law / 2026-law calibration: committed headline regression control.

The 2026/2026 cell reproduces the frozen v2--v5 headline artifacts.  The two
off-diagonal cells isolate the mechanical law substitution from nuisance-scale
recalibration *within this model*.

== Results

The diagonal comparison remains material: v2 is 23.0907 pp in the 2024/2024
cell versus 14.9016 pp in the 2026/2026 cell, and v3 at `Delta=0.25` is 16.5159
pp versus 8.6825 pp.  The diagonal central modeled-positive share is 55.5332%
versus 45.0780%.

Holding the 2024-calibrated nuisance scales fixed, substituting 2026 law for
2024 law moves the central modeled-positive share from 55.5332% to 36.0724%,
v2 from 23.0907 to 12.0629 pp, and v3 at `Delta=0.25` from 16.5159 to 4.5562
pp; v4 feasibility rises from 45/104 to 85/104 grid points.  Holding the
2026-calibrated scales fixed gives the same qualitative result: the modeled-
positive share moves from 59.7184% to 45.0780%, v2 from 28.1125 to 14.9016 pp,
v3 from 19.3563 to 8.6825 pp, and v4 feasibility from 36/104 to 70/104.

Thus the mechanical law substitution is material under either calibration
anchor.  Recalibration offsets part of that movement, which is why the diagonal
2024/2024-to-2026/2026 change is smaller than either fixed-calibration v2 or v3
law substitution.  The exact decomposition is path-dependent because the
model is nonlinear.

The one-sided v5 conclusion is more robust: `eta*=0` at `Delta=0` in all four
cells.  Its conditional taxable-income-weighted MTR envelope still varies, from
7.6523--11.5982% in the 2026-law/2024-calibration cell to 10.1944--12.6557% in
the 2024-law/2026-calibration cell.

== Interpretation

The 2x2 grid separates statutory-law substitution from nuisance recalibration
algebraically inside the specified pseudo-filer model.  It does **not** identify
a causal or population-level additive contribution of the 2024-to-2026 law
change.  Population/frame, tax-event, tax-unit, pseudo-unit, and rank mismatches
remain unresolved, and the two decomposition paths need not assign the same
share to law substitution versus recalibration.  P1-C18 therefore remains a
reproduced sensitivity result rather than an identified, causal, or policy-
usable estimate.

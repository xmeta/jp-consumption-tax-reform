Audit date: 2026-09-14.

This audit separates three objects that must not be conflated:

. structural uncertainty from unobserved population/rank/tax-unit bridges;
. sampling uncertainty in published NSFCW aggregate moments; and
. model uncertainty in the official household-level construction of annual tax.

The current Paper 1 LPs address the first object conditionally on the realized
published aggregate vector. They do not provide sampling confidence regions.

== Official sources retained

The audit uses four Statistics Bureau sources retained in `data/raw`:

* `STAT-F71561-DESIGN`: 2024 NSFCW sample design;
* `STAT-NSFCW-2024-INCOME-ASSET-ESTIMATION-METHOD`: point-estimation and
  weighting method for income/assets results;
* `STAT-NSFCW-2024-ANNUAL-NONCONSUMPTION-METHOD`: construction of annual
  tax/social-insurance amounts; and
* `STAT-NSFCW-2024-RESULTS-PAGE-20260914`: official results-page snapshot on
  the audit date.

== Sampling evidence

The sample-design document describes a stratified multi-stage survey. Its
Table 11 reports *design-stage expected* standard-error rates. The document
states that these forecasts scale the prior survey's standard-error rates by
the square root of the ratio of prior/current approximate aggregated household
counts and assume unchanged coefficients of variation.

For nationwide total households, the Table 11 forecast for annual income is
0.5%. This number is not an achieved standard error for F71561's detailed
household-type x equivalized-income-decile x income-tax cells.

The same document states that achieved standard-error rates are to be computed
using the bootstrap and published later. The current official results-page
snapshot lists the sample-design, result-estimation, usage-note and annual
non-consumption-estimation materials, but no achieved bootstrap standard-error,
variance/covariance or replicate-weight release corresponding to the detailed
F71561 moments used by Paper 1.

The result-estimation document supplies the point estimator and aggregation
weights. It documents adjustment factors, propensity-score integration for the
monitor survey and household-distribution calibration. It does not supply a
cell-compatible variance/covariance matrix or replicate weights for the Paper 1
F71561 inputs.

== Tax-imputation evidence

The annual non-consumption-expenditure method states that annual tax and social
insurance are not collected directly on the annual-income/assets questionnaire.
They are constructed household by household from survey items.

For income tax, the construction applies income deductions and the tax schedule,
estimates family/dependency relationships, omits some deductions when the
survey lacks the necessary information, and applies stated assumptions to
interest/dividend taxation. The method does not publish a probability model,
standard error or confidence interval for this tax construction.

Consequently, the published F71561 income-tax cells are official imputed
statistics, but their imputation uncertainty cannot currently be translated
into statistically justified cell intervals from the public materials retained
here.

== Decision on interval moments

An interval restriction such as

[stem]
++++
M^- \le M \le M^+
++++

would be appropriate only if its bounds can be tied to uncertainty information
that is compatible with the same published moments. The current public evidence
does not support such bounds for the detailed F71561 inputs used by the LP.

In particular, applying the broad 0.5% annual-income design forecast to tax
cells would silently assume both portability across estimands and an
unpublished dependence structure. An arbitrary percentage sensitivity would be
a researcher-chosen stress test, not sampling inference. Paper 1 therefore does
not label such a perturbation as sampling-aware and does not recompute its LP
under a pseudo-confidence band.

== Reopen condition

Reopen statistical propagation when an official release or authorized
microdata route provides enough information to map sampling uncertainty to the
same moments used by the LP, for example:

* achieved cell-compatible standard errors with a justified joint construction;
* a covariance estimator adequate for simultaneous moment uncertainty; or
* replicate weights / bootstrap replicates from which the required joint
  uncertainty can be reconstructed.

For tax-imputation uncertainty, a defensible propagation additionally requires
an official or otherwise justified stochastic model/sensitivity specification
for the imputation inputs rather than treating the published construction as a
random draw without evidence.

Until then, the paper reports structural/model-contingent envelopes conditional
on the realized published moments and explicitly leaves statistical confidence
coverage unclaimed.

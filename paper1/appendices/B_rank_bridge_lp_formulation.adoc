[appendix]
== Mathematical formulation of the v2-v5 compatibility LPs

This appendix gives a manuscript-level specification of the v2-v5 linear
programs.  It is a mathematical restatement of the frozen pre-specifications
and current implementations; it does not alter any feasible set, objective,
grid, or numerical result.

The four versions share one grouped-data and pseudo-scenario core.  We define
that core once and then state v2-v5 as restrictions or objective changes.

=== Indices and fixed scenario set

Let stem:[d=1,\ldots,10] index F71561/pseudo rank deciles,
stem:[q=1,\ldots,10] index NTA rank deciles, and
stem:[j=1,\ldots,25] index ordered NTA total-net-income classes.
The pseudo-filer scenario index is stem:[s\in\mathcal S], with
stem:[|\mathcal S|=13].  The fixed scenario set is

`central`, `f71911_topcode10`, `size_lower`, `size_upper`,
`business_proportional`, `other_wage_split2`,
`no_social_deduction_proxy`, `nta_salary_social`,
`nta_salary_dependents`, `pension_head_merge`,
`pension_age_split_separate`, `pension_member_split`, and
`pension_member_split_f71551`.

No scenario is selected or removed after observing an LP result.
=== NTA grouped-data constants and normalization

For income class stem:[j], let stem:[A_j] be the Table 2-2(1) population count
and stem:[P_j] the positive-self-assessed-balance count.  Define

[stem]
++++
N=\sum_{j=1}^{25}A_j,\qquad
a_j=A_j/N,\qquad p_j=P_j/N.
++++

Let stem:[C_j=\sum_{k\le j}a_k], with stem:[C_0=0], and define the ten
normalized NTA rank bins stem:[B_q=[(q-1)/10,q/10]].  The population mass from
class stem:[j] overlapping rank bin stem:[q] is the fixed quantity

[stem]
++++
h_{jq}=\max\{0,\min(C_j,q/10)-\max(C_{j-1},(q-1)/10)\}.
++++

Thus stem:[\sum_q h_{jq}=a_j] and stem:[\sum_j h_{jq}=0.1].  The construction
uses only the ordering and published grouped counts; it does not assume a
within-class income density.

The aggregate NTA positive-self-assessed-balance rate is

[stem]
++++
\bar b=\sum_jp_j={\sum_jP_j\over N}.
++++
=== Pseudo-filer scenario constants

For each decile-scenario pair stem:[(d,s)], the pseudo construction supplies
three constants used by the LPs:

* stem:[u_{ds}], the modeled annual-income-tax-positive share;
* stem:[m^T_{ds}], the taxable-income-weighted mean statutory MTR;
* stem:[m^L_{ds}], the income-tax-liability-weighted mean statutory MTR.

To make the weighting primitives explicit, index the retained pseudo tax units
within stem:[(d,s)] by stem:[i].  The construction retains a generated tax unit
when its modeled pre-basic-deduction income is positive or its modeled ordinary
income-tax liability is positive.  Let stem:[w_i] be the approximate household
count attached to the source leaf, stem:[x_i=\max\{X_i,0\}] the nonnegative
modeled taxable income, stem:[\ell_i=\max\{L_i,0\}] the nonnegative modeled
ordinary national income-tax liability, and stem:[\rho_i] the statutory
marginal rate.  Then the committed scenario constants are

[stem]
++++
m^T_{ds}=
{\sum_i w_i x_i\rho_i\over\sum_i w_i x_i},\qquad
m^L_{ds}=
{\sum_i w_i\ell_i\rho_i\over\sum_i w_i\ell_i},
++++

with a zero value when the corresponding denominator is zero, and

[stem]
++++
u_{ds}={\sum_i w_i\mathbf 1\{L_i>0\}\over\sum_i w_i}.
++++

These ratios are computed before the LP.  The LP therefore mixes fixed
scenario-level constants; it does not introduce a ratio objective.
=== Common decision variables and constraints

For every F decile and scenario introduce mixture weights
stem:[\lambda_{ds}\ge0] satisfying

[stem]
++++
\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\lambda_{ds}=1\qquad\forall d.
++++

The pseudo modeled-positive rate and the two MTR functionals are therefore

[stem]
++++
r_d^{PF}=\sum_s\lambda_{ds}u_{ds},\qquad
\theta_d^T=\sum_s\lambda_{ds}m^T_{ds},\qquad
\theta_d^L=\sum_s\lambda_{ds}m^L_{ds}.
++++

For each NTA class/rank overlap introduce positive-balance mass
stem:[y_{jq}] with

[stem]
++++
0\le y_{jq}\le h_{jq},\qquad
\sum_{q=1}^{10}y_{jq}=p_j\qquad\forall j.
++++

The positive-balance mass in NTA rank bin stem:[q] is

[stem]
++++
g_q=\sum_{j=1}^{25}y_{jq}.
++++

These constraints preserve each published grouped-class positive total while
leaving its unobserved within-class rank allocation free inside the observed
population overlap.
For v3-v5, introduce the population rank coupling stem:[T_{dq}\ge0] and the
positive-balance transport mass stem:[z_{dq}\ge0].  Their common constraints
are

[stem]
++++
\sum_qT_{dq}=0.1\quad\forall d,\qquad
\sum_dT_{dq}=0.1\quad\forall q,
++++

[stem]
++++
0\le z_{dq}\le T_{dq}\qquad\forall d,q,
++++

and

[stem]
++++
\sum_dz_{dq}=g_q=\sum_jy_{jq}\qquad\forall q.
++++

The NTA positive-balance rate transported to F decile stem:[d] is

[stem]
++++
r_d^B=10\sum_qz_{dq}.
++++

The factor 10 converts positive mass in an F rank bin of population mass 0.1
into a within-bin rate.  The transport constraints imply

[stem]
++++
{1\over10}\sum_dr_d^B=\sum_qg_q=\sum_jp_j=\bar b.
++++

Define the average absolute rank-displacement cost

[stem]
++++
W(T)=\sum_{d=1}^{10}\sum_{q=1}^{10}|d-q|T_{dq}.
++++
=== v2: exact-rank symmetric compatibility

v2 fixes exact same-rank correspondence.  In the common transport notation this
is the restriction

[stem]
++++
T_{dq}=0\ (d\ne q),\qquad T_{dd}=0.1,
++++

which implies stem:[z_{dd}=g_d] and

[stem]
++++
r_d^B=10g_d=10\sum_jy_{jd}\equiv r_d^{NTA}.
++++

The implementation algebraically eliminates stem:[T] and stem:[z] in this
special case.  With stem:[0\le\varepsilon\le1], v2 imposes

[stem]
++++
-\varepsilon\le r_d^{PF}-r_d^{NTA}\le\varepsilon
\qquad\forall d
++++

and solves

[stem]
++++
\varepsilon_2^*=\min\ \varepsilon.
++++

At each fixed budget in the pre-specified grid

[stem]
++++
\varepsilon\in\{0,0.025,0.05,0.075,0.10,0.125,0.15,0.175,0.20,
0.225,0.25,0.275,0.30\}
++++

and at stem:[\varepsilon_2^*], feasibility is checked and each endpoint
functional defined below is separately minimized and maximized over this same
feasible set.
=== v3: rank-relaxed symmetric compatibility

v3 retains the common transport system and imposes the rank budget

[stem]
++++
W(T)\le\Delta,
++++

where the pre-specified grid is
stem:[\Delta\in\{0,0.25,0.5,1,2,3,4,5\}].  For each fixed stem:[\Delta],
with stem:[0\le\varepsilon\le1], the symmetric bridge restriction is

[stem]
++++
-\varepsilon\le r_d^{PF}-r_d^B\le\varepsilon
\qquad\forall d.
++++

The minimum-discrepancy frontier is

[stem]
++++
\varepsilon_3^*(\Delta)=\min\ \varepsilon
\quad\text{subject to the common constraints and }W(T)\le\Delta.
++++

The zero-discrepancy threshold problem instead fixes stem:[\varepsilon=0] and
solves

[stem]
++++
\Delta_0^*=\min\ W(T)
++++

subject to the common constraints and the zero-discrepancy bridge.  If that
problem is infeasible under unrestricted coupling, no finite threshold is
reported.  At each frontier point, stem:[\varepsilon] is fixed at
stem:[\varepsilon_3^*(\Delta)] before the 84 conditional endpoint objectives
are solved.
=== v4: fixed rank/discrepancy budget surface

v4 does not optimize stem:[\varepsilon].  It reuses the v3 common transport
constraints and fixes both budgets on the pre-specified Cartesian grid

[stem]
++++
\Delta\in\{0,0.25,0.5,1,2,3,4,5\},
++++

[stem]
++++
\varepsilon\in\{0,0.025,0.05,0.075,0.10,0.125,0.15,
0.175,0.20,0.225,0.25,0.275,0.30\}.
++++

For each of the 104 pairs, v4 solves the feasibility problem

[stem]
++++
\min\ 0
++++

subject to the common constraints,
stem:[W(T)\le\Delta], and
stem:[-\varepsilon\le r_d^{PF}-r_d^B\le\varepsilon] for every stem:[d].
For every feasible pair the same endpoint functionals are minimized and
maximized separately.  No endpoint is defined for an infeasible pair.

=== v5: one-sided tax-flow compatibility

v5 retains the common grouped-data and transport constraints but replaces the
symmetric bridge by one directional inequality.  With
stem:[0\le\eta\le1], for every stem:[d],

[stem]
++++
r_d^B-r_d^{PF}\le\eta.
++++
For every fixed
stem:[\Delta\in\{0,0.25,0.5,1,2,3,4,5\}], v5 solves

[stem]
++++
\eta^*(\Delta)=\min\ \eta
++++

subject to the common constraints, the one-sided restriction, and
stem:[W(T)\le\Delta].  There is deliberately no mirror inequality restricting
stem:[r_d^{PF}-r_d^B].

The zero-violation threshold fixes stem:[\eta=0] and solves

[stem]
++++
\min\ W(T)
++++

subject to the common constraints and stem:[r_d^B\le r_d^{PF}] for every
stem:[d].  At each frontier point, stem:[\eta] is fixed at
stem:[\eta^*(\Delta)] before the endpoint objectives are solved.

=== Endpoint functionals and the 84 solves

For every feasible fixed-budget or frontier feasible set, define the following
42 scalar quantities:

* ten stem:[\theta_d^T] taxable-income-weighted MTRs;
* ten stem:[\theta_d^L] liability-weighted MTRs;
* ten stem:[r_d^{PF}] pseudo modeled-positive rates;
* ten NTA bridge rates, stem:[r_d^{NTA}] in v2 or stem:[r_d^B] in v3-v5;
* the equal-decile overall taxable-income-weighted MTR
  stem:[\bar\theta^T=10^{-1}\sum_d\theta_d^T];
* the equal-decile overall liability-weighted MTR
  stem:[\bar\theta^L=10^{-1}\sum_d\theta_d^L].

Each scalar quantity is minimized once and maximized once.  Therefore the
endpoint family contains exactly stem:[42\times2=84] LP objectives.
=== Canonical linear-program form

After choosing the version and fixing any external budget values, collect the
active decision variables into a vector stem:[x].  For v3, for example,

[stem]
++++
x=(\{\lambda_{ds}\},\{y_{jq}\},\{T_{dq}\},\{z_{dq}\},\varepsilon).
++++

The common equalities are the scenario simplices, NTA class positive-mass
conservation, both transport margins, and NTA-rank positive-margin
conservation.  Variable bounds contain non-negativity, the overlap capacities
stem:[y_{jq}\le h_{jq}], and the unit-range bound on an optimized common
relaxation.  The inequality blocks contain stem:[z_{dq}\le T_{dq}], an optional
rank budget, and the version-specific symmetric or one-sided bridge.

Every frontier, feasibility, threshold, or endpoint solve can therefore be
written as

[stem]
++++
\min_x\ c^\top x
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
A_{eq}x=b_{eq},\quad A_{ub}x\le b_{ub},\quad \ell\le x\le u.
++++

A maximum endpoint is implemented as minimization with the sign of stem:[c]
reversed.  For a feasibility solve stem:[c=0].  For a minimum-discrepancy
frontier stem:[c] selects stem:[\varepsilon] or stem:[\eta]; for a zero-gap
threshold stem:[c] is the coefficient vector of stem:[W(T)].  The endpoint
vectors select one of the 42 linear functionals above.

This block representation is also the implementation structure: v2 constructs
stem:[(\lambda,y)] directly; v3 adds stem:[(T,z)] and optional epsilon; v4
reuses the v3 matrices with fixed budgets; and v5 reuses the v3 common matrices
while replacing the two symmetric bridge rows per decile with one directional
row.
=== Analytical aggregate-rate floor for v3

The v3 symmetric discrepancy has a lower bound that does not require solving
the full rank-transport LP.  Let

[stem]
++++
m_d=\min_{s\in\mathcal S}u_{ds}.
++++

Because stem:[r_d^{PF}] is a convex mixture of the scenario rates,
stem:[r_d^{PF}\ge m_d].  The symmetric bridge implies
stem:[r_d^B\ge r_d^{PF}-\varepsilon], while the transport construction implies
stem:[r_d^B\ge0].  Hence every feasible v3 solution must satisfy

[stem]
++++
r_d^B\ge\max\{0,m_d-\varepsilon\}.
++++

Summing across deciles and using
stem:[\sum_dr_d^B=10\bar b] gives the necessary aggregate/non-negativity
condition

[stem]
++++
\sum_{d=1}^{10}\max\{0,m_d-\varepsilon\}\le10\bar b.
++++

Equivalently,

[stem]
++++
\sum_{d=1}^{10}\min\{m_d,\varepsilon\}
\ge\sum_{d=1}^{10}m_d-10\bar b.
++++

The smallest stem:[\varepsilon] satisfying this scalar water-filling condition
is the unrestricted nonnegative-rate analytical floor.
Dropping only the per-decile non-negativity refinement yields the still simpler
mean-rate bound

[stem]
++++
\varepsilon\ge {1\over10}\sum_dm_d-\bar b.
++++

For the committed v3 inputs,

[stem]
++++
{1\over10}\sum_dm_d=0.307486,\qquad
\bar b=0.220795,
++++

so the simple aggregate-rate lower bound is 0.086691, or 8.6691 percentage
points.  Solving the scalar non-negativity condition above gives 0.086825, or
8.6825 percentage points.  The independent committed water-filling artifact
matches the full v3 LP minimum for every pre-specified
stem:[\Delta\ge0.25] through stem:[\Delta=5].

The analytical calculation is a lower bound implied by the fixed pseudo
scenario set, the NTA aggregate positive-balance rate, and nonnegative
transported row rates.  It is not a fitted parameter, confidence bound,
measurement-error estimate, or claim that the two source events are identical.
The full LP remains necessary to determine whether grouped-rank and transport
restrictions add incompatibility above this aggregate floor; at
stem:[\Delta=0] they do, while on the positive pre-specified v3 grid they do
not raise the optimum above 8.6825 percentage points.

=== Interpretation and source-of-truth boundary

The pseudo event is modeled ordinary annual income tax greater than zero.  The
NTA event is positive self-assessed return balance after credits and source
withholding.  The populations, units, and income concepts also differ.  Thus
stem:[\varepsilon] and stem:[\eta] are compatibility budgets under explicit
bridge models, not sampling-error or causal parameters.

The frozen pre-specification files remain the implementation contracts.  This
appendix is intended to make those contracts reconstructible from the
manuscript without requiring Python inspection; any future change to a
pre-specified feasible set or objective requires a separate scientific change,
not a documentation edit.

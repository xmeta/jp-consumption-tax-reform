[abstract]
--
We study the identification limits of reconstructing Japanese personal
income-tax marginal rates from public aggregate data when filer microdata are
unavailable for a reproducible analysis. Rather than mapping household
aggregates directly into a synthetic filer distribution, we separate population
selection,
tax-liability information, and marginal-rate assumptions.

For the first-stage filing bridge, the 2024 survey mother population is treated
as a published 2020-Census-based frame benchmark rather than as a directly
observed 2024 person set. We derive a reproduced robustness function in an
explicit total untracked mismatch budget, stem:[C_U], and a general break-even
budget stem:[C_U^*(A)] for any externally supplied admissibility threshold
stem:[A]. The actual value of stem:[C_U] is not identified, so the analysis
does not claim an unconditional filing-rate lower bound.

The model-contingent evidence is organized as a three-level compatibility
hierarchy rather than as a sequence of model versions.  Under exact-rank,
symmetric compatibility (v2 / P1-C14), the minimum common cross-system,
cross-concept rate discrepancy is 14.9016 percentage points.  Relaxing the
unobserved rank correspondence while retaining symmetric compatibility
(v3-v4 / P1-C15-P1-C16) lowers the minimum discrepancy to 8.6825 percentage
points at stem:[Delta=0.25], after which further rank freedom through the
unrestricted stem:[Delta=5] endpoint does not lower it.  Zero symmetric
discrepancy remains infeasible.  On the separately pre-specified fixed-budget
surface, 70 of 104 budget pairs are feasible, every feasible point retains
positive-width overall MTR envelopes, and the overall MTR bounds are unchanged
across the positive pre-specified rank budgets at a fixed `epsilon`.

The third level changes the compatibility question using the audited tax-flow
ordering.  Under tax-flow-consistent one-sided compatibility
(v5 / P1-C17), the minimum directional-violation budget is stem:[eta^*=0]
already at exact same rank and at every pre-specified rank budget.  A positive
symmetric discrepancy can therefore coexist with zero one-sided violation: the
symmetric and directional designs test different restrictions rather than
providing competing estimates of one quantity.

Because the primary pseudo-filer results apply 2026 statutory law to 2024
aggregates, we also run a 2x2 tax-law-year benchmark that separates applied law
from nuisance-scale calibration.  Holding the 2024-calibrated scales fixed,
switching from 2024 to 2026 law moves the v2 symmetric minimum from 23.0907 to
12.0629 percentage points and the v3 `Delta=0.25` minimum from 16.5159 to
4.5562; holding the 2026-calibrated scales fixed gives 28.1125 to 14.9016 and
19.3563 to 8.6825 percentage points respectively.  `eta*=0` at `Delta=0`
survives all four cells.  The decomposition is model-contingent and
path-dependent, not causal identification, but it shows that law substitution
itself is materially consequential rather than merely being absorbed by
recalibration.

All three compatibility levels remain model-contingent rather than an estimated
rank map, confidence region, causal bound, or identified filer MTR.

Other marginal-rate quantities remain sensitivity-only, recovery-only, or
not identified. A machine-readable claim registry prevents calibrated or
unreproduced results from being presented as reproduced identified facts.
--

[abstract]
--
We study what can be learned about Japanese personal income-tax marginal
incidence from public aggregate data when filer microdata are unavailable for
a reproducible analysis.  Rather than mapping household aggregates directly
into a synthetic filer distribution, we separate population selection,
tax-liability information, and marginal-rate assumptions.  For the first-stage
filing bridge we derive an explicit robustness frontier in the unresolved count
of frame-incompatible final-return filers.  Under deliberately adverse
allowances for births, international inflows, and deaths, the recovered
`score_power=2.0` prior is rejected whenever the unresolved count is below
{stage1-critical-cnr} persons.  Other marginal-incidence quantities remain sensitivity
only or not identified.  A machine-readable claim registry prevents recovered
or calibrated results from being presented as reproduced identified facts.
--

[IMPORTANT]
====
This manuscript distinguishes reproduced identified results from recovered
prior-run evidence.  The claim-level authority is
`data/claim_registry.csv`.
====

== Identification framework

Let stem:[M] denote the realized published aggregate vector and let
stem:[\pi] denote a latent distribution over tax units, income-source states,
and tax-law states compatible with those moments.  In the analysis below,
stem:[M] is conditioned on as fixed after publication.  Sampling variation in
the survey estimators and uncertainty in the official tax-imputation procedure
are separate statistical/model-uncertainty layers and are not encoded by the
width of stem:[\Theta(M)].

Define the feasible set

[stem]
++++
\Pi(M)=\{\pi\ge0:
A\pi\in\mathcal C(M),\;
B\pi=b,\;
\text{population and tax-law restrictions}\}.
++++

For a policy-relevant functional stem:[\theta(\pi)], its identified set is

[stem]
++++
\Theta(M)=\{\theta(\pi):\pi\in\Pi(M)\}.
++++

The lower and upper endpoints are obtained by optimization over the feasible
set, not by selecting one convenient latent distribution.
They are structural or model-contingent endpoints conditional on the realized
published moments; they are not a sampling confidence set and carry no claimed
frequentist coverage for the survey or tax-imputation process.

=== Reference selections are not truth

Maximum-entropy, generalized-cross-entropy, pseudo-filer, or other calibrated
constructions can be useful reference selections inside or near a feasible
region.  They are not interpreted as the true joint distribution unless the
data identify them.

=== Population selection

The F71561-to-NTA bridge is decomposed conceptually into

[stem]
++++
\text{candidate person}
\rightarrow
\text{Table 2-1 Final-return processing-row person}
\rightarrow
\text{positive self-assessed balance}.
++++

This paper focuses on making each selection step explicit rather than absorbing
the entire mismatch into a fitted filer distribution.

== Limitations and data improvements

The principal limitation is not numerical optimization. It is the mismatch
between public household aggregates and the filer-level population needed for
filer-level marginal-rate identification.

=== Untracked frame mismatch

The 2024 F71561 mother population is estimated from the 2020 Census frame
rearranged to 2024 municipality boundaries. It is therefore not a directly
observed 2024 set of persons that can be intersected mechanically with the NTA
Table 2-1 Final-return processing-row benchmark.

We leave all additional overlap loss beyond the deliberately tracked allowance
as stem:[C_U]. This quantity can include:

* non-resident persons in the Table 2-1 processing-row benchmark outside the resident/Census bridge;
* remaining 2020-to-2024 population timing mismatch;
* survey eligibility and household-frame differences;
* other unmeasured population-definition mismatches.

The reproduced result is therefore a robustness function in stem:[C_U], not an
identified point value or unconditional lower bound.

Public NTA tables examined for this study do not provide a compatible
resident/non-resident cross-tab of Table 2-1 Final-return processing-row persons.
The FY2024 national annual report's explicit non-resident table belongs to the
withholding-income-tax section and reports payment amounts and withholding tax,
not a residence-status split of Table 2-1 persons. The NTA also reports 920,649
statutory-report information records concerning payments to non-residents
supplied to foreign tax authorities in FY2024, but that is a record count rather
than a deduplicated final-return-person bound and is not used to set stem:[C_U].

=== Sampling and imputation uncertainty

The LP and sensitivity results condition on the realized published aggregate
vector.  Their width represents structural/model-contingent uncertainty under
the stated constraints; it does not incorporate sampling confidence coverage
for F71561 or a probability distribution for the official income-tax
imputation.

The official 2024 NSFCW sample-design document reports design-stage expected
standard-error rates for broad aggregates.  For example, the nationwide
all-household annual-income forecast is 0.5%.  The document explicitly describes
these as forecasts based on the prior survey and says achieved standard-error
rates are to be calculated by bootstrap and published later.  The 0.5% figure
is therefore not an achieved standard error for the detailed F71561
household-type x decile x imputed-income-tax moments used here.

The official income/assets estimation document supplies point-estimation
weights, calibration and propensity-score integration, while the annual
non-consumption-expenditure document supplies the household tax-construction
rules.  In the official materials audited as of 2026-09-14, we do not have a
cell-compatible achieved variance/covariance or replicate-weight release for
these F71561 inputs, and the tax-construction method provides no stochastic
imputation-error distribution.

.Sampling-aware robustness status
[cols="2,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Layer | Status | Current treatment
| Structural bridge uncertainty | represented | LP/frontier/sensitivity analysis conditional on published moments
| Broad NSFCW sampling precision | design forecast only | diagnostic; not transferred to F71561 tax cells
| Detailed F71561 sampling uncertainty | `NOT_AVAILABLE_FOR_CURRENT_LP` | no synthetic stem:[M^-,M^+] interval
| F71561 tax-imputation uncertainty | `NOT_AVAILABLE_FOR_CURRENT_LP` | official assumptions documented; no invented probability interval
| Suppression / rounding | separately documented where possible | not relabelled as sampling uncertainty
|===

Accordingly, we do not perturb every F71561 tax cell by an arbitrary percentage
and call the result sampling-aware.  Such a perturbation would be a researcher-
chosen stress test, not a confidence construction.  The evidence and reopen
conditions are recorded in
`research/income_tax_partial_identification/sampling_imputation_uncertainty_audit.adoc`.
If compatible achieved standard errors plus a defensible joint uncertainty
construction, covariance information, or replicate weights become available,
statistical propagation can be added without changing the structural taxonomy.

=== Data that would materially tighten identification

The highest-value additional data would be:

. a directly comparable 2024 person-frame count or micro-level crosswalk
  between the survey mother population and tax-return population;
. a resident/non-resident count of FY2024 Table 2-1 Final-return processing-row persons on a
  conceptually compatible basis;
. anonymized filer microdata or sufficiently detailed tabulations linking
  taxable-income components, deductions, filing status, and tax liability;
. a direct or statistically linkable crosswalk between F71561-style
  equivalized disposable-income rank and NTA total-net-income rank, so the
  model-contingent rank assumptions used by P1-C14 through P1-C17 can be
  replaced by evidence;
. directly comparable person/tax-unit tabulations linking rank, filing or
  processing status, calculated annual tax, tax credits, source withholding,
  and positive self-assessed return balance, so the transition from positive
  calculated tax to positive return balance can be measured directly rather
  than represented only by event ordering or symmetric compatibility budgets;
. public cross-tabs connecting household income deciles to tax-unit
  characteristics without synthetic assignment;
. repeated-year equivalents permitting validation of stability and transport.

P1-C13 requires more than 22 percentage points of common relaxation under
both pre-specified v1 decile-weight schemes.  The subsequently reproduced NTA
25-income-class filing-status table allows P1-C14 to condition on substantially
richer NTA-side income-rank information; under the separately pre-specified
same-rank bridge, its minimum common relaxation is 14.9016 percentage points.

P1-C15 then weakens exact same-rank matching with a separately pre-specified
rank-displacement budget.  At the first positive grid point,
stem:[Delta=0.25], the minimum discrepancy falls to 8.6825 percentage points
and remains there through unrestricted stem:[Delta=5].  The same 8.6825-point floor is reproduced independently from the NTA
aggregate positive-self-assessed-balance rate and the lower envelope of the
fixed pseudo modeled-annual-tax-positive scenario set, with nonnegative
transported decile rates imposed.  This is a cross-concept floor: it cannot be
interpreted as pure rank mismatch or pure measurement error because the two
positive events differ.  Zero discrepancy remains
infeasible even with unrestricted rank coupling.

P1-C16 holds the discrepancy budget fixed instead of minimizing it and
evaluates the Cartesian product of the already committed v3 stem:[Delta] grid
and v2 stem:[epsilon] grid.  Seventy of 104 points are feasible.  Every
feasible point retains positive-width overall MTR envelopes.  Among the
positive pre-specified rank budgets, all taxable- and liability-weighted MTR
endpoints are identical for stem:[Delta=0.25,0.5,1,2,3,4,5] at a fixed
stem:[epsilon], while the envelopes widen materially as stem:[epsilon] is
relaxed.  Some transported-NTA decile-rate endpoints do continue to widen with
stem:[Delta], so this is an MTR-envelope statement rather than full saturation
of the transport model.

P1-C17 then changes the compatibility question after the separate NTA
tax-flow audit.  Rather than penalizing the two positive-event rates in both
directions, v5 imposes only the tax-flow-consistent direction
stem:[r_d^{B}-r_d^{PF}\le\eta].  The minimum directional-violation budget is
stem:[\eta^*=0] at all eight pre-specified rank budgets, and the separate
zero-violation minimum-rank-cost solve is feasible already at
stem:[\Delta=0].  The post-result slack audit sharpens the interpretation:
at exact same rank five deciles can bind but none must bind over the full fixed
frontier set, and at every positive rank budget all ten can bind while none must
bind.  Thus stem:[\eta^*=0] is a compatibility/falsification result, not evidence
that the directional inequalities are uniformly tight.  The positive v3/v4
symmetric discrepancy therefore does not imply a violation of the subset
direction; the findings answer different questions.

This further sharpens the data priority.  Better rank linkage is still
valuable for replacing model-contingent rank transport, but the highest-value
new evidence is now a directly comparable tax-unit denominator that observes
both positive calculated tax and the downstream positive self-assessed balance
by income rank.  Such data would identify the transition from calculated tax
through tax credits and source withholding to the final return balance,
including the persons currently absent from the positive-balance-conditioned
Sample Survey.  It would therefore test the v5 event ordering quantitatively
rather than merely impose it as a directional compatibility condition.

The Issue-133 same-year benchmark adds tax-law year as a separately visible
sensitivity dimension.  Replacing 2026 law with 2024 law while holding the 2024
aggregate inputs and model family fixed moves the v2 minimum symmetric gap from
14.9016 to 23.0907 percentage points, the v3 `Delta=0.25` floor from 8.6825 to
16.5159 percentage points, and the v4 feasible-grid count from 70/104 to 45/104.
The v5 zero directional violation remains feasible at `Delta=0`.  These
contrasts show that the symmetric-gap magnitudes cannot be decomposed into
population/frame, tax-event, rank, pseudo-model, and law-year components from
current aggregates.

Microdata or sufficiently detailed tabulations could also validate or replace
the pseudo modeled-annual-tax-positive calibration and show whether the
positive symmetric v3/v4 gap reflects population/frame mismatch, tax-event
definition, pseudo-model misspecification, tax-law timing, or some combination.
Neither stem:[Delta] nor stem:[epsilon] is estimated by P1-C16, stem:[eta] is not
an estimated transition, withholding, tax-credit, or measurement-error rate in
P1-C17, and P1-C18 does not identify an additive causal contribution of the
2024-to-2026 statutory change.

=== Scope of policy conclusions

The reproduced results in this paper do not establish an optimal consumption
tax rate, an optimal income-tax schedule, or a structural macroeconomic effect.

A later policy-optimization paper may condition on transparent behavioral
ranges and macro models, but such results must be labelled model-contingent
unless those additional parameters are independently identified.

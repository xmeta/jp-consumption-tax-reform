== Marginal-incidence identification status

The stage-1 result does not by itself identify the filer-level statutory
marginal-tax-rate distribution.

=== Reproducible pseudo-filer sensitivity construction

The repository now contains an independently reproducible pseudo-filer
sensitivity core under
`research/income_tax_pseudofiler/`.

The core is generated from repository-tracked official-source-derived inputs:
F71561 household-type/decile aggregates, the F71911 household-size and age
bridge, F71531 tax and social-insurance moments, and versioned 2026 National Tax
Agency statutory parameters.  The raw official source files, source locators,
and SHA-256 values are retained in the repository.

For each decile and sensitivity scenario, the construction allocates aggregate
income components to pseudo tax units, applies the statutory income
transformations and tax brackets, and chooses a nuisance income scale to match
the observed aggregate income-tax liability moment or, at statutory
discontinuities, the nearest achievable moment.

The nuisance scale is a calibration device.  It is not a behavioral,
structural, or causal parameter.

The construction evaluates thirteen explicit scenarios over ten income deciles, including NTA Table 17 salary-class and F71551 pension-age external sensitivities.
The generated outputs and executable tests are regenerated in CI.  Its
reproducibility status is therefore
`SENSITIVITY_ONLY_REPRODUCED`.

This is a reproducibility statement, not an identification statement.

The construction still does not observe:

* filer-level income;
* filer status for the F71561 household cells;
* within-cell joint income and deduction distributions;
* actual household tax-unit partitioning;
* filer-level age assignment;
* the true joint distribution of taxable income and statutory marginal rates.

Accordingly, the generated pseudo-filer MTRs may parameterize ETI or stress-test
scenarios, but they cannot supply the headline identified incidence result and
cannot be treated as point-identified inputs to optimal-policy conclusions.

The historical V6 file `18_income_tax_pseudofiler_mtr.py` remains a separate
`RECOVERY_GAP`.  The status above refers to the new independently
reproducible sensitivity core, not to byte-identical restoration of the lost
historical implementation.

=== Calibration and statutory rounding

The aggregate moment calibration uses a continuous quick-table convention,
matching the historical sensitivity-construction convention.  The repository
then recomputes ordinary national income tax under the statutory rule that
taxable income is rounded down to the nearest JPY 1,000.

These two quantities are reported separately.

The 2026 basic deduction also changes discontinuously at statutory total-income
thresholds.  Some extreme sensitivity scenarios therefore cannot hit the
aggregate liability moment exactly with one nuisance scale.  Such cases are
reported as nearest-achievable sensitivity diagnostics; the tax-law
discontinuity is not smoothed away to improve fit.

=== Pre-specified replacement transport-relaxation frontier

The historical V6 restricted-LP constraint chain remains unreproduced.  We
therefore do not reconstruct its endpoints by reverse engineering recovered
numbers.

Separately, the repository pre-specified a new transport-relaxation LP before
computing its endpoints.  The model uses all thirteen committed pseudo-filer
scenarios and the five exact NTA primary-income-type stage-2 rates.  It imposes
the published NTA primary-type composition while allowing a common absolute
cross-concept discrepancy `epsilon` between each decile's pseudo share with
modeled annual income tax greater than zero and its transported NTA share with
positive self-assessed balance (`申告納税額>0`).

Two decile-weight schemes were fixed before endpoint computation: equal decile
weights and normalized F71561 leaf-household counts.  The minimum feasible
common relaxation and the conditional overall MTR envelopes are generated
directly from the research outputs:

The required minimum common cross-concept discrepancy is therefore large under
both pre-specified weight schemes: approximately 24.19 percentage points under
equal-decile weights and 22.20 percentage points under F71561 leaf-count
weights.  Exact equality at stem:[\varepsilon=0] is infeasible in this model.

This gap is not a standard error or an estimate of measurement error.  It
quantifies compatibility between *different tax events* under the stated
transport model.  On the pseudo side, the event is modeled annual income tax
greater than zero.  On the NTA side, `申告納税額>0` means that a positive
self-assessed balance remains after tax credits and tax already paid through
source withholding.  The available aggregate data do not identify the
withholding/credit bridge between these events.  The Table 2-2(1) population
also differs from the household source and the separate stage-1 final-return
benchmark.  `epsilon` therefore absorbs cross-concept, cross-population, and
model differences together.

The MTR ranges in the table are conditional envelopes at
stem:[\varepsilon^*].  They are reproducible consequences of the
pre-specified transport-relaxation model, but they are not:

* the historical V6 restricted-LP endpoints;
* statistically sharp identified sets;
* confidence intervals;
* causal bounds;
* point-identified filer MTR distributions.

The historical P1-C06 restricted-LP claim consequently remains
`DOCUMENTED_PRIOR_RUN_NOT_REPRODUCED`.  The v1 replacement result is tracked
separately as P1-C13, a model-contingent sensitivity claim.

=== Pre-specified same-rank v2 frontier

After v1 was completed, the repository reproduced a stronger NTA object from
Annual Statistics Table 2-2(1): exact administrative counts for 25 ordered
total-net-income classes by primary income type and filing-status category.
The 125 positive-self-assessed-balance cells also agree exactly with the independently
extracted NTA Sample Survey Table 2 cells.

Before computing v2 endpoints, a second model was pre-specified.  It does not
match NTA and F71561 income amounts in yen.  Instead, it partitions the ordered
NTA Table 2-2(1) population into ten equal rank bins and assumes only that
F71561 decile stem:[d] corresponds to NTA rank decile stem:[d].

When an NTA income class crosses a rank-decile boundary, the number of persons
from that class entering each rank bin is fixed by the grouped population
counts, but the positive-self-assessed-balance persons inside that class may be allocated
arbitrarily across the overlapping rank bins subject to the published class
total.  Thus no proportional-within-class allocation assumption is imposed.

The resulting v2 summary is generated directly from the committed research
outputs:

|===

The minimum common relaxation is 14.9016 percentage points.  On the
pre-specified grid, values through 12.5 percentage points are infeasible and
15.0 percentage points is feasible.

This required relaxation is numerically smaller than in v1, but the comparison
is not an identification ranking.  v2 adds the unobserved same-rank
correspondence between two different population and income concepts:

* F71561 ranks households by equivalized disposable income;
* NTA Table 2-2(1) ranks its tax-administration population by total net income
  etc.

The same-rank bridge is therefore a model assumption, not an observed
crosswalk.  The v2 MTR ranges are conditional envelopes under that assumption,
not statistically sharp identified sets.

In particular, v2 does not establish:

* equality of F71561 and NTA income concepts or yen thresholds;
* equality of household and tax-administration units;
* an observed record linkage between the two systems;
* a confidence interval or causal bound for filer MTR;
* restoration of the historical V6 restricted LP.

P1-C13 remains unchanged, P1-C06 remains unreproduced, and v2 is tracked
separately as P1-C14.

=== Pre-specified rank-transport-budget v3 frontier

P1-C14 still imposes exact correspondence between the two *rank bins*, even
though the underlying populations and income concepts differ.  We therefore
pre-specified a third model before computing its numerical results.  v3
preserves the v2 NTA grouped-income uncertainty but replaces exact same-rank
matching by a population coupling stem:[T_{dq}] between F71561 decile
stem:[d] and NTA rank decile stem:[q].

Both margins of stem:[T] equal 0.1.  Positive self-assessed balance transport is represented
by a separate mass stem:[z_{dq}le T_{dq}], so the model remains a linear
program while preserving each NTA rank bin's positive-self-assessed-balance margin.  The
average absolute decile-index displacement is constrained by

[stem]
++++
W(T)=\sum_{d=1}^{10}\sum_{q=1}^{10}|d-q|T_{dq}\le\Delta.
++++

The complete stem:[Delta] grid was fixed before results at
stem:[0,0.25,0.5,1,2,3,4,5].  Because one decile-index unit corresponds to ten
percentile-rank points in this discretization, `Delta=0.25` permits at
most 2.5 percentile-rank points of *average absolute displacement*.  It is a
sensitivity budget, not an estimate of the true cross-system rank error.
stem:[Delta=5] is the unrestricted-coupling endpoint for two equal ten-bin
margins.

research output:

At `Delta=0`, v3 reproduces v2 exactly:
stem:[\varepsilon_3^*=14.9016] percentage points, including all 84
minimum-relaxation endpoint objectives in the executable regression test.  At
the first positive pre-specified budget, `Delta=0.25`, the minimum common
discrepancy falls to 8.6825 percentage points, a 6.2191-percentage-point
reduction relative to exact same-rank matching.  It remains 8.6825 percentage
points for every larger pre-specified budget through the unrestricted
stem:[Delta=5] endpoint.

This plateau can be reproduced without solving the full rank-transport LP.
Across the fixed thirteen pseudo-filer scenarios, the equal-decile average of
the decile-specific *minimum* pseudo shares with modeled annual income tax
greater than zero is 30.7486%.  The NTA Table 2-2(1) aggregate
positive-self-assessed-balance rate is 22.0795%.  Their
simple average-rate gap is 8.6691 percentage points.  Requiring transported
NTA decile rates to remain nonnegative raises the unrestricted common
discrepancy floor slightly to 8.6825 percentage points; the independent
water-filling calculation in
`rank_bridge_lp_v3_analytical_rate_floor.csv` exactly matches the LP
frontier.  The separately pre-specified stem:[\varepsilon=0] problem remains
infeasible even when rank coupling is unrestricted.

These results narrow the interpretation of the v2 discrepancy.  Allowing a
small amount of rank transport removes much of the additional incompatibility
created by exact same-rank matching, but arbitrary rank transport cannot remove
the residual cross-concept aggregate-rate incompatibility *within the fixed v3
model and pseudo-filer scenario set*.  This does not establish that either public source
is erroneous, nor does it identify a true cross-system rank map.

The raw v3 research artifacts also contain MTR endpoints evaluated exactly on
the minimum-discrepancy frontier.  For positive stem:[Delta] those endpoints
can numerically collapse because minimizing `epsilon` forces the
pseudo-filer mixture to an extreme lower-modeled-annual-tax-positive boundary.  Such collapse
is an optimization-boundary property, not point identification.  P1-C15
therefore promotes the discrepancy frontier and its analytical floor, not a
point-valued filer MTR.

P1-C13 and P1-C14 remain unchanged, and P1-C06 remains unreproduced.

=== Fixed-budget rank-displacement x discrepancy surface v4

The v3 minimum-discrepancy frontier answers how small the common cross-concept
rate discrepancy can be for a given rank-displacement budget.  It does not by itself
show how the MTR envelope behaves at externally fixed discrepancy budgets.
Because the v3 minimum `epsilon` objective can force the pseudo-filer
mixture to an extreme scenario boundary, a numerically collapsed endpoint at
that frontier is not evidence of point identification.

We therefore pre-specified v4 before computing its numerical surface.  v4
leaves the v3 LP constraints unchanged and reuses only grids that had already
been committed in prior specifications:

* the v3 rank-displacement grid
  `Delta in {0,0.25,0.5,1,2,3,4,5}`; and
* the v2 discrepancy grid
  `epsilon in {0,0.025,...,0.30}`.

The Cartesian product contains 104 fixed budget pairs.  All 104 are reported;
infeasible points are retained rather than selected away.  At every feasible
point, the same 84 endpoint objectives used by v2 and v3 are recomputed.

artifacts:

Seventy of the 104 pre-specified budget pairs are feasible.  At
`Delta=0`, the complete fixed-`epsilon` feasibility and
endpoint surface exactly reproduces v2.  For every feasible positive
rank-displacement budget, all taxable-income-weighted and
liability-weighted MTR endpoints are identical across the entire pre-specified
set `Delta={0.25,0.5,1,2,3,4,5}` at a fixed
`epsilon`.

For example, at `epsilon=0.10`, the positive-`Delta` overall
taxable-income-weighted MTR envelope is 10.5552--11.7415%, and the
liability-weighted envelope is 11.4432--12.6219%.  At
`epsilon=0.30`, the corresponding envelopes widen to
10.0884--12.8680% and 10.8806--13.7776%, respectively.  Every feasible v4
point has strictly positive overall MTR width.

Thus, on the pre-specified grid, once the first positive rank-displacement
budget is admitted, further rank freedom does not widen the MTR envelope; the
remaining envelope width is primarily governed by the externally imposed
discrepancy budget `epsilon`.  This is a conditional sensitivity
statement, not an estimate that `Delta=0.25` is the true rank mismatch or
that `epsilon` is a measured error parameter.

The saturation statement is also deliberately limited to the MTR objectives.
Some decile-specific transported-NTA positive-self-assessed-balance-rate endpoints continue
to widen as stem:[Delta] increases.  v4 therefore does not imply that the
entire transport solution is invariant once `Delta>=0.25`.

P1-C16 records this fixed-budget surface as a reproduced model-contingent
sensitivity result.  It is not a confidence region, posterior region, causal
bound, statistically sharp identified set, or point-identified filer MTR.
P1-C13 through P1-C15 remain unchanged, and P1-C06 remains unreproduced.

=== Static liability versus behavioral incidence

Observed F71561 income-tax liability moments can support static,
first-order liability calculations under explicitly stated accounting
assumptions.  This is the meaning of `READY_STATIC_ONLY`.

Static liability incidence does not identify:

* filer selection under a policy change;
* taxable-income elasticities;
* general-equilibrium earnings responses;
* dynamic tax revenue;
* the joint welfare effect of a VAT-income-tax reform.

Those quantities remain `NOT_READY`.

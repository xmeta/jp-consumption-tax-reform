== Scope and contribution

This paper studies a narrower question than the companion consumption-tax
policy project: what can be learned about reconstructing Japanese personal
income-tax marginal rates from public aggregate data when filer microdata are
not available for a fully reproducible analysis?

The paper does not estimate an optimal VAT rate, an optimal macro policy mix,
or a structural GDP effect.  Those questions require additional behavioral and
macro identification and are deliberately outside this submission.

=== Contributions

First, we treat population mismatch as an identification problem rather than
silently mapping household aggregates into filer microdata.

Second, we distinguish observed moments, partial identified sets, reference
selections, sensitivity constructions, recovered prior-run evidence, and
quantities that remain not identified.

Third, we derive a reproducible stage-1 population / processing-row overlap
robustness diagnostic using the exact NTA Table 2-1 Final-return processing row
as a public benchmark.  The diagnostic leaves the unresolved number of
frame-incompatible persons in that processing-row benchmark explicit rather
than imputing a filing rate.

Fourth, after pre-specifying each design before inspecting its endpoints, we
organize the rank-based evidence as a compatibility hierarchy.  The first tier
imposes exact-rank correspondence and symmetric cross-concept compatibility
(v2 / P1-C14).  The second relaxes rank correspondence while retaining the
symmetric criterion, reporting both the minimum-discrepancy frontier and a
fixed-budget MTR sensitivity surface (v3-v4 / P1-C15-P1-C16).  The third keeps
the rank transport but replaces symmetric closeness with the tax-flow-consistent
one-sided ordering implied by calculated tax, credits, withholding, and final
self-assessed balance (v5 / P1-C17).  This hierarchy separates the effect of
relaxing rank assumptions from the effect of changing the compatibility
question; its outputs remain model-contingent diagnostics rather than identified
MTR sets.

Fifth, every substantive statement is tied to a machine-readable claim
registry, making overstatement detectable during revision.

== Relation to the literature

=== Partial identification

The methodological stance follows the partial-identification literature:
empirical content should be characterized by the set of parameter values
consistent with data and maintained assumptions, rather than by forcing a
point estimate when the data do not support one.  This perspective is
developed by <<manski2003>>, surveyed by <<tamer2010>>, and updated by
<<kline-tamer2023>>.

Our stage-1 result is especially close in spirit to this literature because the
unobserved population mismatch is left as an explicit nuisance parameter and
the conclusion is stated as a threshold frontier.  The current analysis
conditions on the realized published aggregate vector and does not construct
sampling confidence regions for the latent MTR set.  The distinction between an
identified set conditional on realized moments and statistical confidence
regions follows <<chernozhukov-hong-tamer2007>>.

=== Taxable-income responses and marginal rates

The elasticity-of-taxable-income literature emphasizes that marginal tax rates
and behavioral responses are distinct empirical objects and that ETI can serve
as a sufficient statistic only under additional assumptions; see
<<saez-slemrod-giertz2012>>.

That distinction motivates our refusal to infer behavioral net revenue from a
static liability reconstruction.  The present paper concerns identification of
marginal-rate inputs, not estimation of ETI from aggregate household tables.

=== Tax-benefit microsimulation

Microsimulation is a standard tool for evaluating tax incidence,
redistribution, poverty, and policy reforms.  <<bourguignon-spadaro2006>>
discuss the interpretation and limits of redistribution microsimulation, while
<<sutherland-figari2013>> describe the data, policy coding, and validation
architecture of EUROMOD.

For Japan, <<ohno2023>> develops a household-microdata microsimulation of
personal income taxation and highlights applications including marginal tax
rates and distributional burden analysis.

Our contribution is complementary rather than a substitute for those models.
When suitable household or filer microdata are available, microsimulation can
apply tax law at the unit level.  Our question is what remains defensibly
identified when redistribution of the underlying microdata is unavailable and
the reproducible researcher instead observes public aggregate tables from
different populations.

=== Aggregate data, ecological bounds, and data combination

The closest methodological literature asks what can be learned when the joint
microdata needed for an individual-level estimand are unavailable.  Classical
ecological bounds, beginning with <<duncan-davis1953>>, constrain latent cell
counts using observed aggregate margins rather than selecting a unique
individual-level table.  More recently, <<moon2026>> obtains partial-identification
bounds from marginal aggregate distributions by optimization.  Our maintained
problem differs in one important respect: the Japanese household and tax
tables can differ in population, unit, income concept, and tax event, so a
within-population ecological bound cannot by itself bridge the sources.

Statistical matching and data-combination methods instead study variables split
across sources with no common record.  <<dorazio-dizio-scanu2006>> surveys the
identifying role of matching assumptions.  In econometrics, <<fan-sherman-shum2014>>
derive sharp bounds rather than forcing point identification when outcomes and
covariates are observed in separate data sets, while
<<dhaultfoeuille-gaillac-maurel2025>> characterize a sharp identified set for an
unlinkable-data problem using optimal transport.  These papers motivate the
separation between an observed margin and an unobserved coupling.  Our
rank-bridge calculations do not statistically match records, estimate a latent
linkage, or claim sharpness for the full Japanese tax problem; they range over
explicitly restricted couplings as model-contingent sensitivity analyses.

=== Couplings, transport, and dependence uncertainty

Optimal transport provides a natural language for distributions with fixed
margins and admissible couplings; see <<galichon2016>> for the economic
formulation.  The v3-v5 matrix stem:[T_{dq}] is exactly such a finite coupling:
both margins are fixed at 0.1 because each source has been reduced to ten equal
rank bins.  The cost

[stem]
++++
W(T)=\sum_{d,q}|d-q|T_{dq}
++++

is average absolute displacement in decile-index units, and the model restricts
stem:[W(T)\le\Delta].  Crucially, stem:[\Delta] is not estimated from linked
records, not a confidence radius, and not an empirically calibrated Wasserstein
radius.  It is a pre-specified sensitivity budget.  Nor is this a partial-
transport model: all normalized population mass is coupled.  The analysis also
does not select the cost-minimizing coupling as the estimated truth; it
optimizes the reported functional over all couplings satisfying the stated
budget and the additional tax constraints.

This logic is related to Fréchet--Hoeffding reasoning: fixed marginals alone
leave dependence unresolved, and sharp generic bounds range over compatible
joint distributions; see <<nelsen-et-al2004>>.  Our transport sets add
rank-displacement and tax-system restrictions, but population and concept
mismatch prevent us from relabeling their endpoints as generic
Fréchet--Hoeffding bounds or as statistically sharp identified sets.

=== Calibration, entropy, and synthetic populations

There is also a distinct literature that chooses one joint reconstruction
consistent with aggregate controls.  <<ireland-kullback1968>> studies
contingency tables with fixed marginals using a relative-entropy criterion.
<<deville-sarndal1992>> develops survey calibration weights satisfying known
auxiliary totals, and <<beckman-baggerly-mckay1996>> uses iterative proportional
fitting plus seed microdata to construct synthetic populations.  These methods are valuable when
a representative synthetic population is the target, but matching the margins
does not make the selected joint distribution uniquely identified by those
margins.

The paper therefore treats maximum-entropy, calibrated pseudo-unit, and
pseudo-filer constructions as reference selections or sensitivity devices, not
as identification results.  Conclusions are promoted to identified claims only
when they hold over the maintained admissible population set; otherwise the
selection rule or robustness budget remains visible in the claim.  This is also
why we do not invoke generic distributionally robust optimization as a separate
identification argument: the uncertainty sets here are purpose-built feasible
couplings and tax constraints, not probability-distance balls around an
estimated joint distribution.

=== Methodological boundary

The paper does not claim a new optimal-transport algorithm, statistical-matching
estimator, ecological-inference estimator, or synthetic-population generator.
Its methodological contribution is narrower: it combines public Japanese
aggregate tax and household tables while keeping population mismatch,
dependence uncertainty, reconstruction selection, and rank-transport
sensitivity as distinct layers with different evidence statuses.  In
particular, stem:[T_{dq}] is an unobserved coupling variable and
stem:[\Delta] indexes a sensitivity family; neither is evidence of record
linkage or of the true cross-source rank correspondence.
